## What this issue covers {#intro}

Most work with a Bot has two ends: handing it the material, and getting the result back. Hand over material without context and the Bot has to guess. Ask for a vague result and what comes back is hard to check or pass on.

The official docs have a page called "Files and results" for exactly this: how to attach files, how to ask for results, how to open and share pages, and how to find past work in the Library. This issue follows that order and turns it into five tips you can use right away.

::: callout The one thing to remember
When you send material, say what each file is and how to use it. When you ask for a result, say what form it should take and how you'll judge it. A new page is private to you, so decide who should see it before you share it.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it.

### Tip 1: Give every attachment a label {#tip1}

Use the attachment control or drag files into the composer. You can also paste images and links. Common formats such as images, audio, video, PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, CSV, and JSON are supported. In the desktop app, the limits are:

| Item | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Attachments per message | 20 |
| Images | 25 MB each, 200 MB total per message |
| Documents, audio, and other files | 250 MB each |
| Video | 1 GB each |

A file over its limit is flagged as too large and left out; send it in another message. Encrypted, damaged, or unusual files may not be readable, so export them as PDF, CSV, plain text, or an image first. On your phone, you can also send something straight to a Bot from another app through the system share sheet.

What matters more than the file itself is telling the Bot what each file is and how to use it:

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I've attached two files:
1. The PDF is our signed [expense policy].
2. The spreadsheet is [this month's expense claims].
Check every claim against the policy, cite the relevant policy section for each exception, and return a new spreadsheet plus a short summary. Don't modify the originals.
:::

### Tip 2: Name the result and how you'll judge it {#tip2}

The docs recommend naming the artifact and its acceptance criteria up front, for example:

::: props
- Document: With headings and source links
- Spreadsheet: With columns and formulas defined in advance
- Slide deck: With speaker notes
- Folder: With screenshots and logs
- Message: A draft that hasn't been sent
- Recommendation: A short answer first, then the evidence
:::

For consequential work, ask the Bot to split the result into five parts: facts found in source systems, assumptions or inferences, actions already completed, actions waiting for approval, and unresolved questions. When you want changes, ask it to revise **the existing artifact** rather than making a new copy each time.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Write up this week's progress on [a project] as a document:
1. Add a source link after every claim.
2. Use five sections: facts found, inferences, what's done, what's waiting for my approval, and open questions.
3. End with a separate list of anything you couldn't verify.
When I send feedback later, edit this same document instead of creating a new file.
:::

### Tip 3: Ask your Bot for a page {#tip3}

A page is something the Bot builds for you to read: a report, a memo, a dashboard, a tracker, or an explainer. It can hold text, tables, charts, and controls such as filters and switches. Ask in chat and say what the page is for and what should be on it. When it's ready, the Bot posts a card with the page's title and **Open**.

::: steps
- Open it | In the desktop app, click the card. The page fills the window and a smaller chat panel stays open beside it.
- Change it | Type in the panel labeled **Message about** and the page's title. The Bot edits the same page. Filters and switches only change what you see; nothing you do on the page is saved.
- Rename it | Click the title of a page you made, type a new name, and press Enter. Press Escape to keep the old name.
- Spot updates | When the Bot updates a page, the earlier card in the chat says **Updated below**. Click it to open the latest version.
:::

Pages is rolling out gradually and isn't available on Enterprise teams yet. If you don't see it, update to 0.60.0 or later on your computer and 1.14.0 or later on your phone. A routine can also create or update a page on a schedule.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Make a page that compares [these three quotes]:
1. A table of price, delivery time, and support terms.
2. A filter to show only the options under [my budget].
3. A short recommendation at the end, with the reasons behind it.
Post the card when it's ready, and keep the page private to me for now.
:::

### Tip 4: Pick the right audience before you share {#tip4}

A new page is private to you. In the desktop app, open the page and click **Share** at the top, or choose **Share…** from the page's menu in the Library, then choose who can view it:

| Option | Who can open the page |
| --- | --- |
| People you invite | You and the teammates and groups you add. On a personal account, this is Only you |
| Your team's name | Everyone on your Cursor team |
| Anyone with the link | Anyone, with no sign-in needed |

Everyone you share with can read the page and use its controls; only the Bot can change it. A page shared with anyone who has the link gets a fixed address on `pages.grok.bot` that anyone can open without signing in, so **share this way only when the page holds nothing private**. Public sharing is off on Enterprise teams. To narrow access, open Share again and choose a smaller option. You can also review every page you've shared under Grok Bot Pages in the Cursor dashboard, change its visibility, or delete it, and deleting it cuts off everyone with the link.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I'm about to share [a page] with a partner outside our team. Before I do, please check it:
1. List anything that shouldn't leave the team, such as names, emails, phone numbers, amounts, or internal links.
2. If you find any, fix or remove them on this same page and tell me what you changed.
3. Don't change the sharing settings. I'll do that myself once I've checked.
:::

### Tip 5: Find past work in the Library {#tip5}

Every Bot has a **Library**. Click the Bot's name at the top of the chat and open the Library tab. It lists the pages, files, and apps the Bot made and the images, videos, and links it sent in your conversation, grouped under **Today**, **This week**, and **Older**. Things you sent yourself aren't listed.

::: props
- Quick search: In the desktop app, press Cmd/Ctrl+K and type a page's title
- Pin: Choose Pin from a row's menu to keep it at the top under Pinned
- Filter by type: In the desktop app, choose Pages, Media, Links, Files, Apps, or Pinned
- Shared mark: A page shared with your team or anyone with the link shows a mark on its row
:::

Once you've connected Google Drive, OneDrive, SharePoint, Gmail, or Outlook, the Bot can also save results straight to your drive or attach them to an email draft. Note that saving to Google Drive always creates a new file, even when one with the same name exists, while OneDrive and SharePoint refuse an existing name unless you tell the Bot to overwrite it.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Take stock of your Library for me. Report only, don't change anything:
1. List the pages and files you made in the past week, with one line on what each is for.
2. Mark the ones that are shared with anyone.
3. Suggest the three I use most so I can pin them.
:::

## Limits worth knowing {#limits}

::: steps
- Attachment caps | The desktop app allows 20 attachments per message, and files over their limit are left out.
- Pages isn't everywhere yet | It's rolling out gradually and isn't available on Enterprise teams yet.
- Be careful with public links | Anyone with the link can open the page without signing in, so keep private material off it.
- Only the Bot edits pages | Filters and switches on a page aren't saved; ask the Bot to change the content.
- Images and video on request only | Image creation isn't used for charts or tables, so ask for a page. Clips run 1 to 15 seconds, 6 by default, at 480p unless you ask for 720p.
- Screenshots aren't enough | For fast-changing data, keep a source link or export as well.
:::

## Case study: how a fictional "Weekly Reporter" delivers a shareable report {#case}

Continuing our fictional cast (not anyone's real setup): an operations lead owes her team a data report every Friday. She used to copy numbers out of several sheets, build the charts, and write the takeaways herself. Now she hands it to a Bot called "Weekly Reporter".

| Step | What she does | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hand over material | Sends two exported spreadsheets and says what each is | The originals stay untouched |
| Ask for the result | Asks for a page with charts and a filter | Claims carry evidence; unverified items are listed separately |
| Revise | Gives feedback in the panel beside the page | Same page, no new copies |
| Share | Shares with her own team only | No public link |
| Archive | Pins the report in the Library | Next week, the same page gets updated |

### Highlight: one page, updated weekly, always there for the team {#case-flow}

All she does is send the material, check the result, and share once.

::: flow
- Me | Send the sheets and say what they are | person
- Weekly Reporter | Checks the data and builds the page
- Weekly Reporter | Posts the page card
- Me | Give feedback and share with the team | person
- Team | Open the page and filter | person
---
- Me → Weekly Reporter: Two sheets, one for signups and one for conversions
- Weekly Reporter → Me: The page is ready; I couldn't verify two numbers
- Me → Weekly Reporter: Mark those two, and add a filter by channel
- Weekly Reporter → Me: Updated on the same page
- Me → Team: The weekly report page is shared with you
:::

Later she had Weekly Reporter update the same page every Friday on a schedule, so the team always opens the same link and sees the latest numbers.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You're this "Weekly Reporter". When I send you weekly data, follow these rules:
1. First tell me which files you read and what each one is.
2. Build the result as a page with key metrics, charts, and a filter by channel.
3. Back every claim with evidence, and list anything you couldn't verify separately.
4. Update the same page each week instead of making a new one, and don't change its sharing settings.
Repeat the rules back to me first.
:::

## Community picks: common ways to use it {#community}

Here are some common setups that are easy to start with. They're general ideas, so swap in your own details.

### Start from a screenshot {#c1}

Send a photo of an error, a whiteboard, or a paper receipt along with what you want done. It's much faster than describing it in words.

### A quote comparison page {#c2}

Send several quotes at once and have the Bot build a page with a table and a recommendation you can open in the meeting.

### A living tracker {#c3}

Have the Bot build a launch tracker with tasks, owners, and statuses, then ask it to update the same page as things move.

### Save results straight to your drive {#c4}

With a drive connected, have the Bot save finished files to a folder you name, so you skip downloading and re-uploading.

### Illustrations and short clips {#c5}

When you need a quick illustration or a few seconds of video, describe the subject, style, and shape. Anything that needs exact text or brand assets still belongs in a design tool.
