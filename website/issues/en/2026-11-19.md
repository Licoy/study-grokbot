## What this issue covers {#intro}

In earlier issues we covered how to create your own Bots and how to share a Bot as a template. But often the Bot you want already exists: one that watches the web for mentions of your brand, one that reviews a pitch deck, one that turns meeting notes into a recap deck.

The official Bot Marketplace is where those ready-made Bots live. This issue breaks it into five tips: where to find Bots, how to read a Bot's page, how importing works, how to make a Bot your own, and how to clean up when one isn't a fit.

::: callout The one thing to remember
Read the Bot's page before you import. What you get is your own copy. Answer its questions, check the routines it brings, and keep sending and publishing behind approval.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it.

### Tip 1: Know where the marketplace is, and browse by category {#tip1}

The marketplace is at [x.ai/bot/marketplace](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace). In the GrokBot app, open **Connect apps** in the sidebar and choose **Bot templates** to open it in your browser.

It's sorted by role, with **Featured** and **From Grok Bot Team** at the top:

::: props
- Engineering: research and fact-checking, acceptance checks before you ship, wrapping an API in a plugin
- Sales: finding prospects, following up after meetings, scoring sales calls
- Marketing: content ideas for search and AI answers, cutting clips from long recordings, rewriting copy
- Design: design critique, Figma build specs, alt text for images
- Personal: household reminders, a plant care log, profile pictures
- Recruiting & People, Product, Operations: interview scheduling, stress-testing product ideas, office tasks, and more
:::

If nothing jumps out, ask your primary Bot. Describe what you need and have it shortlist a few candidates.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I'm looking for a ready-made Bot to [one job, like checking what competitors shipped each week].
Look through the GrokBot Bot Marketplace, pick the two or three closest matches, and tell me:
1. the one job each of them does;
2. which apps each one needs;
3. whether any of them sends or publishes on its own.
Only compare them. Don't import any Bot.
:::

### Tip 2: Read the Bot's page before you import {#tip2}

Open any Bot and its page shows the creator, a short description, and four kinds of content:

| On the page | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| memories | Facts it already knows |
| skills | Playbooks it can run |
| routines | Jobs that run on their own |
| integrations | Apps it can use |

Below that are its full instructions. A good marketplace Bot usually spells out three things: its one job, what it won't do, and when it stays quiet. For example, the instructions for [Overheard](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/overheard) say it only looks for third-party mentions of your name, brand, and URLs, stays quiet on days with nothing worth reporting, and never posts for you.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Here is the page of a Bot I'm thinking of importing: [paste the page text]
Please check:
1. which apps and access it needs;
2. which routines it brings and how often they run;
3. whether its instructions send, publish, or pay for anything on their own.
Tell me in three to five sentences whether it's worth importing.
:::

### Tip 3: Import Bot gives you a copy of your own {#tip3}

Choose **Import Bot** on a Bot's page to add your own copy of it to your account. The official docs make a few points clear:

::: steps
- You get the configuration only | You get the Bot's setup, such as its identity, description, skills, and routines. You never get the creator's computer, logins, or conversation history.
- Not made by Grok Bot | Marketplace and template Bots are made by other users, not by Grok Bot. Adding one accepts the third-party bot terms.
- Your team may set limits | On the Teams and Enterprise plans, admins control whether members can publish Bot templates publicly.
:::

A template link someone sends you works the same way: open the preview, choose **Add to Grok Bot**, and you get your own copy. For sharing a template yourself, see [Issue 9](/issues/2026-11-09/).

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I just imported [Bot name] from the marketplace. Before you start, tell me:
1. your job, and what you won't do;
2. the skills and routines you came with, and whether each routine is on;
3. which apps you need me to connect.
Don't run any routine yet.
:::

### Tip 4: Answer its setup questions, then make it your own {#tip4}

Many marketplace Bots ask you a few questions on their first run. Overheard, for example, first asks which names, brands, and URLs to watch, then creates its daily routine. Its instructions say the routine starts switched off, and it asks you once whether to turn it on.

Once you've answered, adapt it to the way you work:

::: steps
- Tell it your rules | Your time zone, language, when to deliver results, and what it must ask about first. Say it in chat and ask it to remember.
- Check its routines | Confirm each routine's schedule and trigger, and have it turn off the ones you don't need.
- Connect only what it needs | You don't have to connect every app it lists. Start with the fewest and add more once it works.
- Run it once before letting go | Have it do the whole job once by hand. Let the routine run on its own only when you like the result.
:::

To maintain its memory and routines, see [Issue 6](/issues/2026-11-06/) and [Issue 2](/issues/2026-11-02/).

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Please adjust yourself with these rules:
1. My time zone is [time zone], and write all results in [language];
2. Send me results every weekday at [9 a.m.], and skip days with nothing worth reporting;
3. Ask me before any send, publish, or delete.
Remember these, then list all of your routines and when they run.
:::

### Tip 5: Hide or delete a Bot that isn't a fit, and clean up the shared computer {#tip5}

If an imported Bot turns out not to fit, there are two ways to put it away:

| What you do | What happens | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hide from sidebar | It leaves the main list but keeps its work and history, and its routines keep running. Restore it from Hidden Bots at the bottom of the sidebar | You may need it later |
| Right-click in the sidebar → Delete | Erases its profile, conversations, memory, routines, and attachments. It can't be undone | You're sure you're done with it |

Hiding is not the same as turning it off. To make it fully quiet, have it turn off its routines first, then hide it.

Also keep in mind that all your Bots share one cloud computer, and files and sign-ins there aren't kept separate per Bot. After you delete a Bot, the files and sign-ins it left on the computer remain and need a separate cleanup.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I'm going to delete [Bot name]. Before I do, please:
1. list the files and folders it created on the shared computer;
2. list the websites it signed in to;
3. tell me whether it saved any shared facts for my other Bots.
Only make the list. Don't delete anything until I confirm.
:::

## Limits you should know {#limits}

::: steps
- Hiding isn't pausing | A hidden Bot still runs its routines. Turn the routines off if you want it to stop.
- Review other people's work | Marketplace Bots are made by other users. However good the instructions look, watch its first run.
- Approvals still apply | An imported Bot works like one you built and asks before risky actions. Sending, publishing, and paying stay behind approval.
- Secrets stay out of chat | When it needs a sign-in or an API key, use Vault, a Secure Form, or a secure secret card. Never paste it into chat.
- The computer is shared | An imported Bot can use the files and sign-ins on your shared computer too, so decide whether you trust it before you import.
:::

## Case study: a fictional independent bookstore picks a "word-of-mouth watcher" {#case}

A fictional scenario (not anyone's real setup): the owner of an independent bookstore wants to know when people mention the store online, but doesn't want to check a dozen sites every day.

| Step | What they did | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pick | Asked the primary Bot to compare two or three mention-tracking Bots in the marketplace | Compare only, no import yet |
| Review | Read each page: which apps, how many routines, whether it posts | Chose the one that clearly says it never posts for you |
| Import | Chose Import Bot and answered its setup questions: store name, website, the owner's pen name | Left the extra sources off for now |
| Adapt | Asked it to remember: report on weekday mornings only, and stay quiet when there's nothing new | Turned the routine on only after one good run |

### Highlight: review first, turn the routine on last {#case-flow}

The owner didn't let it run on its own right after importing. They had it do one run by hand first and checked the results.

::: flow
- Me | Describe the kind of Bot I want | person
- Primary Bot | Shortlist and compare marketplace Bots
- Word-of-mouth watcher | Ask setup questions and do a test run
- Me | Turn on the routine after checking the result | person
---
- Me → Primary Bot: Find me a Bot in the marketplace that watches what people say about the store online. Don't import it yet.
- Primary Bot → Me: I found three. The second one only sends a digest and never posts, which fits best.
- Word-of-mouth watcher → Me: Test run done. Two new mentions, both with links. The daily routine is set up but not on yet.
- Me → Word-of-mouth watcher: Looks good. Turn it on, and skip weekends.
:::

A month later, the owner imported an alt-text Bot from the marketplace to describe new poster images the same way: read the page, then do a test run.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You are this "word-of-mouth watcher". From now on, follow these rules:
1. Only report other people's mentions of [store name] and [website], not our own posts;
2. Include the original link and a one-line summary for each one;
3. On days with nothing new, don't message me, and never reply or post for me.
Repeat the rules back to me first.
:::

## Community picks: creations from the marketplace {#community}

This time we picked a few Bots by community creators from the official marketplace, to show how others do one job well. The descriptions come from their pages, so read them again yourself before you use one.

### dr eggbot: a Bot that designs Bots {#c1}

By Lauren Tan. [dr eggbot](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/dr-eggbot-v2) asks you a few preference questions, then creates Bots built around one job each, with the things they won't do written down. It's a good place to start when you're not sure how to split up the work.

### Tradbot: household things don't slip {#c2}

By Claire Vo. [Tradbot](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/tradbot-2) watches your personal email and calendar for school forms, bills, and RSVPs, and drafts the reply, but never sends without you.

### Meeting Recap Deck: from meeting notes to a recap deck {#c3}

By Krista Letz. [Meeting Recap Deck](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/echo) turns your meeting notes into a recap deck in your slide template, and says it never invents a quote.

### Haggle Bot: find the subscriptions worth cutting {#c4}

By Daniel Gartshein. [Haggle Bot](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/haggle-bot) inventories your SaaS spend, finds unused seats, duplicates, and cheaper alternatives, and drafts vendor counters for your review. It never spends, signs, or sends without you.

### Imogen: alt text for images {#c5}

By Kent C. Dodds. [Imogen](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace/bots/imogen) writes brief, copyable alt text focused on the most important part of an image, so images are accessible to blind people.
