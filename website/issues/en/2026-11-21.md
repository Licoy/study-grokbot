## What this issue covers {#intro}

[Issue 9](/issues/2026-11-09/) covered bringing a Team Bot into Slack. Plenty of companies work in Microsoft Teams instead, and a Team Bot can join you there too: teammates mention it in a channel, add it to a group chat, or message it directly, without switching apps.

Getting a Bot into Teams takes three gates: a team admin turns it on, the Bot's owner submits it, and a Microsoft Teams admin approves it. After that, each member links their own Microsoft account. This issue turns those steps into five tips and ends with a table of the messages you might see and who should handle each one.

::: callout The one thing to remember
A team admin turns it on, the Bot's owner connects it and sends the approval link to the Teams admin, and after approval you wait for setup to finish. Members link their account the first time they talk to it.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it. Dashboard settings and approvals in the Teams admin center must be done by the right admin in person; the prompts only ask your Bot to organize and draft.

### Tip 1: Get three things ready first {#tip1}

The official docs list only three requirements, and you need all of them:

::: steps
- A published Team Bot | A draft can't be connected, and you must be its owner or a Manager. Team Bots need a Team or Enterprise plan and at least one other person on your team.
- Microsoft Teams turned on for your team | A team admin does this. See Tip 2.
- A Microsoft account in the right organization | It must belong to the organization that uses Teams.
:::

To create and publish a Team Bot, see [Issue 9](/issues/2026-11-09/). The official advice is to give each Team Bot one job and name it for that job.

::: prompt
I want to bring our Team Bot "[Bot name]" into our company's Microsoft Teams.
Using the official docs, check each of these for me:
1. Whether the Bot is published, and whether I'm its owner or a Manager;
2. Whether a team admin has turned on Microsoft Teams;
3. Which Microsoft account I need.
List anything that isn't ready yet and who should handle it. Only check; don't change any settings.
:::

### Tip 2: A team admin turns on Microsoft Teams first {#tip2}

Only team admins can turn it on, and it takes two steps:

::: steps
- Turn it on | On the Grok Bot page of the team dashboard, under Shared Bots, choose Enable next to Enable Microsoft Teams.
- Grant access in Microsoft | Sign in with a Microsoft Teams or Global administrator account and accept the permissions Microsoft shows.
:::

The row then shows that Microsoft Teams is enabled for your organization, and Bot owners on the team can connect their own Bots. Revoke turns it off. In the same place, Manage Microsoft Teams accounts lists the members who linked a Microsoft Teams account and lets an admin unlink one. Note that even after it's turned on, a Microsoft Teams admin still approves each Bot.

::: prompt
I'm a team admin and I'm about to turn on Microsoft Teams for GrokBot.
Write me a checklist that covers:
1. Where to turn it on and which kind of Microsoft admin account I need;
2. What members and Bot owners still need to do afterwards;
3. Where to turn it off later, or unlink a member's account.
Just the checklist; don't do anything for me.
:::

### Tip 3: Connect the Bot, and hand the approval link to your Teams admin {#tip3}

The owner or a Manager connects the Bot after publishing it:

::: steps
- Find the entry point | Open the Bot's details and find Bring to your team's Microsoft Teams, or choose Manage and look under Channels. Choose Connect Microsoft Teams.
- Sign in with Microsoft | Finish signing in in the browser window that opens. Confirm it's the right account, and pick an organization if you belong to more than one.
- Wait for approval | While the dialog says Waiting on admin approval, a Microsoft Teams admin needs to approve the Bot in the Teams admin center under Teams apps > Manage apps > Pending approval. Microsoft doesn't notify them, so send them the link the dialog shows. Choose Check again right after they approve, or Cancel request to stop.
- Let setup finish | After approval, Microsoft Teams sets up the Bot on its own, which can take up to an hour. The dialog then says the Bot is in Microsoft Teams, with Open in Teams.
:::

To use it in a channel, add it to a team from Microsoft Teams. The dialog lists the teams it has been added to. If a Teams admin declines the Bot, the dialog shows their note. Make the changes they ask for and choose Resubmit.

::: prompt
I just submitted our Team Bot "[Bot name]" to Microsoft Teams, and it's waiting for a Teams admin to approve it.
Draft a message to our Teams admin, [name], that:
1. Explains what the Bot does and who will use it;
2. Includes the approval link: [paste the link from the dialog];
3. Says where in the Teams admin center to approve it.
Keep it short and polite. Draft only; don't send it.
:::

### Tip 4: Members link their account, then use it in channels, group chats, and DMs {#tip4}

The Bot needs to know who you are before it can act for you. The first time you message it, it replies with a Link Account card only you can see. Choose it and sign in with your Microsoft account. You can also link first: open Settings > General > Team Bots and choose Link next to Microsoft Teams account. If it says your admin hasn't turned on Microsoft Teams for your organization yet, ask a team admin.

Once you're linked, here's what the Bot can do in Teams:

| Capability | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Answer in channels and chats | Mention it in a channel of a team it joined, add it to a group chat, or message it directly |
| Read the conversation | It reads the thread it's in, and the channels of the teams it joined |
| Work with files | It reads files attached to posts and sends you files through your OneDrive. Ask for a file in a channel or group chat and it asks you to message it directly instead |
| Act for you | As in Slack, it acts for the person who asked, uses their usage, and asks before risky actions |

To have a Bot join a Teams call and transcribe it, use meetings. See [Issue 12](/issues/2026-11-12/).

::: prompt
@[Bot name] Please read the discussion about [project name] in this channel and summarize:
1. Decisions that have been made;
2. Open questions and who raised them;
3. Next steps.
Reply with the key points under this post only, and don't send any other messages for anyone. Ask me before using any of my apps.
:::

### Tip 5: Renaming, removing, and disconnecting each have different effects {#tip5}

Once the Bot is in Teams, keep these in mind when you change it:

::: props
- Name or picture: After you change them, the dialog says the Bot's name or avatar changed. Choose Update in Teams. If your Teams admin has to approve the change, it shows An update is waiting for your Teams admin
- Remove it from Teams: Choose Remove from Microsoft Teams in the same dialog. This removes the app from your Microsoft Teams organization, and teammates can't reach the Bot there until it's added again
- Disconnect your Microsoft account: This disconnects every Bot you own in that organization, not only this one. Bots already in Teams keep answering there
- Delete the Bot: Deleting a Team Bot also removes it from Microsoft Teams
:::

Before you act, decide what you actually want. Pausing for a while and retiring the Bot for good are very different operations.

::: prompt
Our Team Bot "[Bot name]" has been in Microsoft Teams for a while, and now we want to [rename it / stop using it for now / retire it].
Using the official docs, tell me:
1. Which action to use;
2. Which people and which Bots it affects;
3. Whether a Teams admin has to approve anything again.
Suggestions only; don't do anything yet.
:::

## Bot not in Teams? Read the message, find the right person {#troubleshoot}

The message in the connect dialog usually tells you which gate is stuck. This table follows the official docs:

| Message (short form) | What it means | Who handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microsoft Teams isn't enabled | Your team hasn't turned it on | A team admin chooses Enable Microsoft Teams on the dashboard |
| Publish this Bot first | The Bot is still a draft and must be published to your team | The owner or a Manager chooses Publish, then connects |
| Waiting on admin approval | Your Teams admin hasn't approved it | Send the dialog's link to the Teams admin, then choose Check again |
| Your Teams admin declined | The Bot was declined | Read their note, make changes, and choose Resubmit |
| Teams didn't accept the app | Your organization doesn't allow custom apps | A Teams admin allows them, or you choose Download app package and send it to them to upload |
| Blocked by Teams app policy | An app policy blocks it | A Teams admin allows the app in the Teams admin center |
| Need admin approval | The Microsoft sign-in needs an admin's consent | Send the link to a Microsoft Teams or Global admin, then connect again |
| Microsoft Teams stopped working | The team's connection expired | A team admin chooses Reconnect Microsoft Teams on the dashboard |
| Finishing setup | Approved, and Teams is still setting it up | Nothing to do; it can take up to an hour and finishes on its own |
| A Link Account card | Your Microsoft account isn't linked yet | Choose the card and sign in |

If none of these match, contact support with the Bot's name, the exact message, and your Grok Bot version. For a general troubleshooting order, see [Issue 16](/issues/2026-11-16/).

::: prompt
While bringing our Team Bot into Microsoft Teams, I see this message:
[paste the exact message]
Using the official docs, tell me which step went wrong, who should fix it (me, the Bot's owner, a team admin, or a Teams admin), and which button I choose once it's fixed. Steps only; don't do anything for me.
:::

## Limits you should know {#limits}

::: steps
- Only Team Bots go in | You connect a published Team Bot, as its owner or a Manager. Team Bots need a Team or Enterprise plan.
- Turned on isn't approved | After a team admin turns it on, a Microsoft Teams admin still approves each Bot in the Teams admin center.
- Microsoft won't remind anyone | Teams admins aren't notified when you submit. You send them the approval link yourself.
- Approval isn't instant | Teams finishes setup on its own, which can take up to an hour. Meanwhile you see Finishing setup in Microsoft Teams.
- Disconnecting has a wide reach | Disconnecting your Microsoft account disconnects every Bot you own in that organization.
- Add only what everyone may use | A Team Bot uses the apps, secrets, and files its owner added. The official advice is to add only what everyone you share it with may use.
:::

## Case study: a fictional 30-person consultancy brings a Weekly Report Bot into Teams {#case}

A fictional scenario (not anyone's real setup): a consultancy of about thirty people does all its talking in Microsoft Teams. A project manager built a Team Bot, Weekly Report Bot, that turns channel discussions into a weekly project update, and wants everyone to use it right inside Teams.

| Step | Who | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Publish | Project manager (owner) | Publishes Weekly Report Bot to the team, and adds a colleague as a Manager |
| Turn on | Team admin | Turns on Microsoft Teams under Shared Bots on the Grok Bot page |
| Submit | Project manager | Chooses Connect Microsoft Teams, signs in with Microsoft, and copies the approval link |
| Approve | IT colleague (Teams admin) | Approves it under Pending approval in the Teams admin center |
| Add | Project manager | Waits for setup to finish, then adds the Bot to the Projects team |
| Use | Everyone | Chooses Link Account the first time they talk to it, then mentions it in the channel |

### Highlight: the Bot drafts the approval note, a person sends it {#case-flow}

The only step likely to stall was waiting for the Teams admin. The project manager had their primary Bot draft the note, then sent it to IT themselves.

::: flow
- Project manager | Submits the connection and gets the approval link | person
- Primary Bot | Drafts a note for the IT colleague
- IT colleague | Approves it in the Teams admin center | person
- Weekly Report Bot | Answers teammates in the channel
---
- Project manager → Primary Bot: Weekly Report Bot is waiting for Teams admin approval. Write a short note with this link, but don't send it
- Primary Bot → Project manager: Draft's ready. It covers what the Bot does, who uses it, and where to approve it. Please check it and send it yourself
- IT colleague → Project manager: Approved it under Pending approval
- Project manager → Weekly Report Bot: @Weekly Report Bot Please write up this week's project progress from the channel discussion, and reply under this post only
:::

A week after launch, the project manager renamed the Bot to Project Weekly Bot and chose Update in Teams, watching the dialog: if it showed An update is waiting for your Teams admin, IT would need to approve it once more.

::: prompt
You are our team's "[Bot name]", working in Microsoft Teams. Please follow these rules:
1. Reply only under posts where you're mentioned, and don't post in other channels on your own;
2. When you need to send a file, ask the person to message you directly;
3. Ask the person before using any of their apps.
Repeat the rules back to me first.
:::

## Community picks: getting more from a Team Bot in Teams {#community}

Here are a few ways to use a Team Bot in Teams. The first two come from the official Team Bot best practices; the other three build on the official docs and are offered as ideas.

### One Bot, one job {#c1}

The official advice is to give each Team Bot one job and name it for that job: a Launch Bot, a Copy Bot, a Research Bot. In a Teams channel, a clear name tells teammates who to mention.

### At least two Managers {#c2}

What a Team Bot learns is reviewed by its owner or a Manager, and they are also the ones who connect it to Teams. Add a second Manager so reviews and upkeep don't wait on one person.

### Line up your Teams admin early {#c3}

Microsoft doesn't notify Teams admins, so give your IT colleague a heads-up before you connect. Send the link as soon as you have it, and choose Check again right after they approve.

### Ask in the channel, get files in a DM {#c4}

Keep discussions and summaries under channel posts where everyone can see them. When you need the Bot to make a file, message it directly, and it sends the file through your own OneDrive.

### Share a message lookup table {#c5}

Paste this issue's message table into your team's internal docs. Teammates who hit a message know who to ask, without going to an admin every time.
