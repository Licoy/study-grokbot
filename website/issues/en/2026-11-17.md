## What this issue covers {#intro}

The last dozen or so issues were about putting your Bots to work. But plenty of people use Grok Bot for weeks without really opening settings: routines run a few hours off, the phone buzzes even when they're at their desk, and they find out usage is almost gone only when it runs out.

Most of these take a click or two in settings. This issue turns the official "Settings and notifications" page into five tips: make appearance and language work for you, set your time zone and import memory, get notifications under control, understand usage and resets, and finally a set of shortcuts and deep links.

::: callout The one thing to remember
Cmd/Ctrl+, opens settings and Cmd/Ctrl+/ shows every shortcut. Your time zone decides when routines run, notifications are set per Bot, and you can always just ask your Bot about usage.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it.

### Tip 1: Make appearance and language work for you {#tip1}

Open the account menu and choose **Settings**, or press `Cmd/Ctrl+,`. Settings has the **General**, **Computer**, **Vault**, **Usage & Billing**, and **Updates** panes. Which sections appear depends on your account and rollout, so a missing option doesn't necessarily mean you did something wrong.

::: props
- Light or dark: In **Appearance**, choose **Follow System**, **Light**, or **Dark**
- Accent: **Accent** colors your 1:1 Bot messages and cards with each Bot's color (**Bot color**, the default) or keeps them **Black**
- App language: **Language** offers **Follow System** or one of more than 20 languages, including Simplified and Traditional Chinese. On phones, the follow-device option is labeled **System** and the list is shorter
- Spell check: **Check Spelling While Typing** is on by default and underlines misspelled words as you type
- Microphone: **Microphone** in the **System** section picks the microphone Grok Bot listens to when you talk to it
- Flickering window: If the window flickers or stays blank, turn off **Use hardware acceleration** and choose **Restart Now**. It's on by default on macOS and Windows and off on Linux
:::

A Bot's name, picture, and label aren't in settings. Click the Bot's name at the top of the chat (**View conversation details**) to open its details. Click the name or label to edit it, and click the picture to change the avatar. Personal Bots don't have a description; to change what a Bot does, tell it in chat.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I want to adjust your role. From today, your main job is [a kind of work, like organizing my weekly reading notes].
1. Restate your new job in one sentence.
2. List any old habits you remember that conflict with it.
3. Wait for my confirmation before updating your memory.
:::

### Tip 2: Set your time zone and bring memory over from elsewhere {#tip2}

**Settings → General → Bot** has **Timezone**, and routines use it for their schedules. If your routines run at the wrong time after a trip or a move, check here first. This section also has your Auto-review switch and **Custom Rules**. Grok Bot manages model selection, so there's no model picker.

If you've built up preferences and context in another AI assistant, bring them over with **Import Memory to Grok Bot** in the **Import** section:

::: steps
- Start | Choose **Start Import**.
- Ask the other assistant | Copy the prompt into a chat with your other AI assistant.
- Paste the answer | Paste its answer into the second box.
- Add it | Choose **Add to Memory**. The import goes to your primary Bot and opens that chat.
:::

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I just imported memory from another assistant into you. Please review it:
1. Sort it into three groups: preferences, background, and outdated or uncertain.
2. Flag the items you think may be outdated and need my confirmation.
3. Don't delete or rewrite anything until I've confirmed each item.
:::

### Tip 3: Get notifications under control {#tip3}

Notifications are set per Bot. Turn on **Notifications** for a Bot to get a system or mobile notification when it finishes or is waiting for you. On the phone, the switch is in the Bot's profile. On desktop, ask the Bot to turn its notifications on or off. Group chats don't have the same per-Bot switch.

::: props
- At your desk: While you use the desktop app on macOS or Windows, notifications from Bots you own go to the desktop instead of your phone, and finished replies skip your phone
- Waiting on you: When a Bot needs your input, your phone is notified about 3 minutes later unless you open that Bot, turn off its **Notifications**, or hide it from your sidebar first
- Linux: Notifications are suppressed while Grok Bot is focused, and your phone is notified as usual
- Phone permission: The phone apps ask for notification permission during first run. Both the device permission and the Bot's setting must allow it. On iPhone, push delivery is rolling out gradually
- Sidebar states: **Needs attention** means a question, approval, or handoff is waiting for you, and **Unread activity** means a new result. Use the Bot menu to mark a chat read or unread
- In-app errors: They appear above the composer under **Notifications**. You can dismiss one or clear the list. Clearing a notice doesn't undo the action behind it
:::

Your primary Bot also reaches out on its own: it offers to take on work it spots, and when an app such as Gmail signs you out, it tells you in its chat and offers to reconnect. If that's too many alerts, turn off its **Notifications**. That controls alerts, not the check-ins themselves; to turn those off, contact support.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Help me tidy up notifications:
1. Turn on your own Notifications, and only alert me when a task is done or needs me.
2. List which of my Bots currently show Needs attention, and what each is waiting for.
3. Tell me which Bots could have notifications turned off and why. Don't change any other Bot for me yet.
:::

### Tip 4: Understand usage and make good use of resets {#tip4}

**Settings → Usage & Billing** has up to five sections, always in this order:

| Section | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| My plan | Your plan and a button to manage it, plus **Manage billing** under **Billing** for payment methods and invoices. Enterprise teams see **Managed by your organization** |
| Usage limits | How much of your weekly usage you've used and when it resets. Enterprise members see their spend limit instead |
| Grok Bot usage | A usage chart, **By Bot** or **By source** |
| Additional usage | Usage past your weekly limit: **Credits**, **Buy credits**, **Auto top-up**, and more, depending on your plan |
| Resets | The usage resets you have, each with its expiry date |

Choose **Use reset**, then **Confirm**, and your weekly usage goes back to 0% right away, without waiting for the regular weekly reset. After you use one, you have to wait before you can use another. When you reach your weekly limit, the banner above the message box also offers **Use Reset**. If you have no resets and can refer friends, you may see an option to refer a friend for a free reset.

You can also skip settings and ask in your private chat with a Bot. It tells you where your usage comes from now, how much is used, and when it resets. Only the Bot's owner can ask, and only in a private chat; in a group chat or anywhere else, the Bot declines. When a usage warning appears, **Review Usage** asks your Bot to review it for you.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Please check my usage this week:
1. How much is used and when it resets.
2. Which Bots are using the most.
3. Two or three suggestions, such as routines that could run less often.
Suggestions only; don't pause or change any routine yourself.
:::

### Tip 5: Learn a few shortcuts, then open chats with a deep link {#tip5}

These are the desktop shortcuts most worth remembering. Use `Cmd` on macOS and `Ctrl` on Windows and Linux:

| Action | Shortcut |
| --- | --- |
| Jump to / search palette | `Cmd/Ctrl+K` |
| New Bot | `Cmd/Ctrl+N` |
| New group chat | `Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+N` |
| Find in this chat | `Cmd/Ctrl+F` |
| Sidebar Bot 1 through 9 | `Cmd/Ctrl+1` to `Cmd/Ctrl+9` |
| Add selected text to chat | `Cmd/Ctrl+L` |
| Dictate into the composer | `Cmd/Ctrl+D` |
| Send from anywhere | `Cmd/Ctrl+Enter` |
| Connect apps | `Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+M` |
| Show all keyboard shortcuts | `Cmd/Ctrl+/` |

To open a Bot's chat in one click from a note, a shortcut, or another app, use a deep link: `grokbot://app/v1/prompt?id=<bot-id>&text=<message>`. It opens the Bot's chat with up to 1,000 characters of text in the message box, but it doesn't send it for you, and it adds the text only if the message box is empty. Without `id`, it opens your primary Bot. The Bot's ID is the end of its link: click the Bot's name, choose **Share**, then **Copy link**. Encode the text, for example `%20` for a space.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Help me make a deep link that opens a chat with you in one click:
1. Tell me how to find your Bot ID.
2. Prefill the message box with: [Help me prioritize today's to-do list first].
3. Encode the text correctly, give me the full link, and confirm it only fills in the text and doesn't send it.
:::

## Limits you should know {#limits}

::: steps
- Options vary | Which settings sections you see depends on your account and rollout, so you may not see every option in the docs.
- No model picker | Grok Bot manages model selection, and there's nowhere in settings to choose a model.
- Execution is per computer | **Execution on this computer** is set per desktop, with per-command approval by default. Your personal Auto-review rules are saved to your account and apply on every device.
- Automatic updates restart the app | **Automatic Updates** under **Settings → Updates** is on by default. It installs an update and restarts the app while you're away: the screen is locked or the screensaver is on, the computer has been idle for at least 10 minutes, and Grok Bot isn't busy. Linux `.deb` and `.rpm` installs update through the system package manager and don't show the switch.
- Ask about usage privately | Only the Bot's owner can ask about usage, and only in a private chat, so don't expect an answer in a group chat.
:::

## Case study: a fictional "Writing Helper" on the wrong time zone {#case}

A fictional scenario (not anyone's real setup): a freelance writer has a Bot called "Writing Helper" that uses a routine every morning to summarize the story ideas collected the day before. After moving to another city, the summary started arriving in the middle of the night, and the phone kept buzzing even while the writer sat at the desk.

| Step | What they did | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check the time zone | Timezone under Settings → General → Bot still showed the old city | Routines use this time zone for schedules |
| Fix it | Changed it to the new city's time zone | Ask the Bot to confirm each routine's next run time |
| Tidy notifications | Kept Notifications on only for Writing Helper | Alerts that wait on a reply reach the phone about 3 minutes later |
| Check usage | Asked Writing Helper about this week's usage in their private chat | One frequent routine used the most |
| Slow it down | Changed that routine from hourly to twice a day | Only after the writer confirmed |

### Highlight: done in ten minutes, and the Bot kept working throughout {#case-flow}

From noticing the wrong time to finishing the fix, it took only settings and a few messages. No Bot was paused.

::: flow
- Me | Notices the summary arrives at night | person
- Writing Helper | Checks the time zone and run times
- Me | Fixes the time zone and notifications | person
- Writing Helper | Reports usage and suggests changes
---
- Me → Writing Helper: Why did the summary arrive at night? Tell me which time zone you're scheduling with first
- Writing Helper → Me: Still the old one. Change it in settings and I'll check again
- Me → Writing Helper: Done. While you're at it, which routine used the most this week?
- Writing Helper → Me: The hourly idea scan used the most. I suggest running it twice a day
:::

Afterward, the writer asked Writing Helper to report the previous week's usage every Monday morning, to catch heavy routines early.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You are this "Writing Helper". From now on, follow these rules:
1. Every Monday morning, report last week's usage in our private chat, plus the three routines that used the most.
2. Before changing the time or frequency of any routine, tell me and wait for my confirmation.
3. Only notify me when I need to act. Don't send separate alerts for routine work that finished.
Restate the rules first.
:::

## Community picks: common ways to use it {#community}

This issue collects a few common, easy-to-start ideas. They're general patterns; swap in your own details.

### One color per Bot {#c1}

Keep **Accent** on the default **Bot color** and give each Bot its own avatar, so you can tell at a glance who you're talking to.

### Check the time zone after you travel {#c2}

The first thing to do after a trip or a move is glance at **Timezone**, then ask your Bot to list the next run time of every routine.

### Review memory after importing {#c3}

Right after an **Import Memory**, ask your primary Bot to sort what it received, and remove outdated items one by one.

### Tiered notifications {#c4}

Turn on **Notifications** only for Bots that really need you to step in. For the rest, **Unread activity** in the sidebar is enough.

### Deep links in your notes {#c5}

Turn a few messages you send often into deep links and keep them in your notes or shortcuts, so one click opens the right Bot with the text filled in.
