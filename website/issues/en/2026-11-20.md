## What this issue covers {#intro}

Most earlier issues were about how you use GrokBot yourself. Once a whole team starts using it, the questions change: who gets access, who can share Bots, whether Bots may join meetings or send email, which actions must ask a person first. A team admin settles these for everyone.

This issue takes the admin's point of view and turns the official teams and enterprises docs into five tips and a launch checklist. Members should read it too: most of the greyed-out switches and "disabled by your admin" messages you see come from here.

::: callout The one thing to remember
Confirm your plan and clear the network and privacy blockers first, then set the sharing, capability, and approval switches one by one. Keep Team Rules short, and let Auto-review handle approvals.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it. Dashboard settings must be changed by an admin; the prompts only ask your Bot to organize and check information.

### Tip 1: Know your plan before you decide how to turn it on {#tip1}

How Grok Bot gets turned on depends on the plan:

| Plan | How people get Grok Bot |
| --- | --- |
| Individuals | Included with paid plans, through an individual SuperGrok link, or with a seat on a self-serve Grok Business plan |
| Teams | On by default for every member, with usage following the seat's allowance. There is no switch to turn it off |
| Enterprise | An admin turns it on from the Grok Bot page of the dashboard, for all members or only certain groups with Manage Group Access |

A few details: Grok Bot stays off for teams on Privacy Mode (Legacy) or a legacy request-based plan. On Enterprise, turning it off blocks every member without deleting their computers. Admins on either plan can bring members in with Invite Team on the Grok Bot page. Several settings are Enterprise only and don't appear on self-serve Teams.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I'm a team admin. We have about [number] people on the [Teams / Enterprise] plan.
Using the official Grok Bot docs for teams and enterprises, make me a table of:
1. Whether Grok Bot is on by default for our plan, and how to turn it on or limit it to some people;
2. Which admin settings are Enterprise only;
3. The privacy and billing prerequisites I should confirm first.
Only gather the information. Don't change any settings.
:::

### Tip 2: Clear the network and privacy blockers before rollout {#tip2}

The official docs list what to handle before you roll out:

::: steps
- Move off Privacy Mode (Legacy) | It blocks Grok Bot entirely, and you're prompted to change it before enabling. Check the privacy setting in Team Settings.
- Plan for shared egress addresses | If your company restricts services by source IP, plan for static egress addresses ahead of time.
- Clear your gateway | If member devices sit behind Zscaler or another TLS-inspecting proxy, allow the required domains, including the nested subdomain pattern, and exempt them from inspection before members connect.
- Decide how members sign in | Decide how members sign in to company tools from the computer. Okta and Entra ID have their own setup pages.
- Review inherited policies | Team Rules and the connector policy apply on every plan. Team Auto-review rules and the MCP allowlist are Enterprise only.
:::

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Write a pre-rollout checklist for our IT team, grouped into five areas: privacy setting, egress addresses, proxy exemptions, sign-in method, and inherited policies.
For each item, give:
1. What to confirm;
2. Who owns it;
3. What goes wrong if it's skipped.
Put it in a table, and don't send it to anyone yet.
:::

### Tip 3: Set the sharing rules: who can share, and whether logins are shared {#tip3}

Admins decide how Team Bots and templates get shared from the Grok Bot page. Most of these live under Sharing:

::: props
- Bot sharing: Everyone (the default) or Admins only. With Admins only, members see Only team admins can share Bots and can't use Share. Bots already shared stay shared
- Public template sharing: Whether members can publish Bot templates outside your team. Off keeps sharing team-only, enforced on the server, including for templates that are already public. It starts off on Enterprise teams and allowed on other teams
- Allow Shared Logins: Lets a Team Bot's Managers share their own app logins with everyone. Off by default. When on, everyone who uses the Bot acts through that account, including for changes the Bot makes
- Slack Workspace Access: Lets members of your Slack workspace who aren't on the team use Team Bots in Slack without a linked account, for Bots shared with your organization and similar cases. Their replies use the team's usage. On by default. Slack guests and Slack Connect channels are never included
:::

Manage Bots on the Grok Bot page lists every Bot on your team. Filter by sharing or owner, and change who has access. In the Auto-add column, pick All team or certain groups to add a Bot to their sidebars; members who get a Bot this way can't hide it. Deleting a Bot here removes it and its Slack app for everyone, and can't be undone. For building and sharing a Team Bot itself, see [Issue 9](/issues/2026-11-09/).

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
We're about to open up Team Bots. Compare two sharing policies for me:
A. Everyone can share, but shared logins stay off;
B. Only admins can share, and a few Bots get shared logins.
For each, explain what kind of team it suits and where the risks are, then tell me which fits us: [describe your team]. Advice only for now.
:::

### Tip 4: Decide what Bots may do, switch by switch {#tip4}

A handful of switches decide what members' Bots can do:

| Switch | Where and default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meetings | Under Bot Capabilities | Whether members' Bots can join Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams calls and transcribe them. When off, Bots can't join or read meetings |
| Agent Email | Under Bot Capabilities, off by default | Members can claim an email address only when it's on. Email a Bot writes on its own, including from routines, waits as a draft until the member presses Send |
| Page sharing | Under Sharing, Anyone on the team by default | Can be narrowed to specific teammates or turned off. Pages already shared stay shared |
| Cloud Agents | On the Grok Bot page, on by default | Whether Bots can delegate coding tasks. Turn it off if your team doesn't need it |
| Execution on Local Computer | On the Grok Bot page, Always allow by default | Always allow, Ask every time, or Never allow. The docs recommend Never allow unless Bots have a specific reason to work on member machines |

Connected apps aren't set on the Grok Bot page. Grok Bot follows your team's existing connector policy: a permitted connector is available to every Bot a member runs, and a blocked one shows as Disabled by team admin. Pushing connectors to members isn't available. For how meetings and agent email work, see [Issue 12](/issues/2026-11-12/) and [Issue 10](/issues/2026-11-10/).

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Draft a decision table for our Grok Bot capability switches: meetings, agent email, page sharing, delegating coding tasks, and running on members' own computers.
For each one:
1. What the default is;
2. Whether our [department or line of work] needs it;
3. Whether to turn it on or off, with a one-line reason.
Draft only. Don't change any settings for me.
:::

### Tip 5: Keep Team Rules short, and let Auto-review handle approvals {#tip5}

Team Rules are added on the Grok Bot page. Every member's Bots follow them, and members can't turn them off. The docs advise keeping them short and few, like "never move company data to personal accounts." Rules guide a Bot; Auto-review is what controls approvals:

::: steps
- Enforce Auto-review | Enterprise only, off by default. When on, members can't turn Auto-review off, and Bots always check risky actions before running them and ask for approval when needed. That includes Team Bots in chats where nobody can answer an approval, which otherwise run without Auto-review.
- Auto-review rules | Enterprise only. Add team-wide Ask first and Allow automatically rules with Configure Rules, on top of each member's own rules. Ask first wins when rules conflict.
- What members see | Members see the team rules under Settings → General → Auto-review but can't edit or delete them.
:::

The docs advise turning on Enforce Auto-review before you rely on team Auto-review rules. For the basics of approvals and Auto-review, see [Issue 5](/issues/2026-11-05/).

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Rewrite the team requirements below as no more than five Team Rules, one sentence each, that every member's Bots can follow:
[paste your requirements, for example: never move company data to personal accounts, ask a person before sending anything outside the company]
Then mark which ones work better as an Ask first Auto-review rule than as a plain rule.
:::

## Limits you should know {#limits}

::: steps
- Many switches are Enterprise only | The organization-wide switch, Network Controls, Team Secrets, Enforce Auto-review, team Auto-review rules, and more appear only on Enterprise.
- One member's Bots share one computer | Members are isolated from each other at the hardware level, but all of one member's Bots share a computer. When a workload needs its own computer and credentials, the docs suggest giving it its own user.
- Terminating a computer doesn't remove access | It ends the member's current work, and their next message starts a fresh computer on the same durable disk. To remove access, remove the member from the team or turn off their access.
- Shared logins need care | With Allow Shared Logins on, anyone who talks to the Bot can act as that account. Share only an account that holds nothing sensitive.
- Connectors can't be pushed | You can allow or block them, but not install them or turn them on for members.
:::

## Case study: a fictional 20-person design studio rolls out GrokBot in a week {#case}

A fictional scenario (not anyone's real setup): a design studio of about twenty people on the Enterprise plan. The admin wants one project team to try it first, then everyone.

| Step | What they did | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare | Had a Bot build the pre-rollout checklist from Tip 2 and went through it with IT | Confirm Privacy Mode (Legacy) isn't in use |
| Pilot | Turned on the switch and used Manage Group Access to open it to the project team only | Nobody else is affected yet |
| Set rules | Sharing set to Admins only, shared logins off, local execution set to Never allow | Only three Team Rules |
| Turn on capabilities | Meetings on, agent email off for now | Review after one week |
| Expand | Auto-added the studio's own "project schedule Bot" to everyone in Manage Bots, then opened access to all groups | Read the pilot feedback first |

### Highlight: start small, then open up step by step {#case-flow}

The admin didn't flip every switch at once. A Bot organized the information, and the admin confirmed each setting in the dashboard.

::: flow
- Admin | Explains the team and its concerns | person
- Primary Bot | Builds the checklist and switch recommendations
- IT colleague | Confirms the network and sign-in setup | person
- Admin | Sets each switch and runs the pilot | person
---
- Admin → Primary Bot: We're opening GrokBot to the project team first. List what I need to confirm, and don't touch any settings
- Primary Bot → Admin: Done: privacy setting, egress addresses, proxy exemptions, sign-in method, and inherited policies, each with a suggested owner
- IT colleague → Admin: The proxy now allows and exempts the domains, so the pilot can start
- Admin → Primary Bot: The pilot has run for a week. Summarize everyone's feedback on one page and mark the switches we should change
:::

A week later, based on the summary, the admin turned on agent email and added one Team Rule: "ask a person before emailing anyone outside the studio."

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You're our team's rollout helper. From now on:
1. Only organize information and drafts. Never change any admin settings for me;
2. For every suggestion, name the section of the official docs it comes from;
3. Flag the risk separately for anything that touches member privacy or shared accounts.
Repeat these rules back to me first.
:::

## Community picks: habits admins use {#community}

Here are a few habits for rolling GrokBot out to a team. The first two come from the official Team Bot best practices, and the last three build on the official settings.

### One job per Team Bot {#c1}

The docs advise giving each Team Bot one job and naming it for that job: a Launch Bot, a Copy Bot, a Research Bot. When the name says what it does, members know which one to ask.

### At least two Managers {#c2}

What a Team Bot learns is reviewed by its owner or a Manager. Adding a second Manager means suggestions don't wait on one person.

### One sentence per rule {#c3}

Use Team Rules only for real red lines, one sentence each. Leave the details to each Team Bot's own instructions, and approvals to Auto-review rules.

### Pilot first, then everyone {#c4}

On Enterprise, open Grok Bot to one group with Manage Group Access first, and widen it once things run smoothly. If something goes wrong, only a few people are affected.

### Let a Bot collect the feedback {#c5}

During the pilot, have your primary Bot summarize the issues people raise into one page each week. The admin reads only the summary and decides which switch to change.
