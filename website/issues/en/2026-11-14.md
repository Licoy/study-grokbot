## What this issue covers {#intro}

Most past issues assumed you were at your computer. But a lot of work shows up while you're out: an idea on the commute, a receipt you just photographed, a link you want your Bot to read, or a Bot waiting on an approval from you.

The official docs have a page called "Grok Bot for Mobile". This issue follows a simple order, talk, send, use, tap, and manage, and turns it into five tips you can use right away: talk to your Bot, send it things from the share sheet, let it use your phone's apps, start it from an iPhone shortcut, and handle approvals and routines from your phone.

::: callout The one thing to remember
The phone app connects to the same Bots and the same cloud computer, and work keeps going when the app is closed. Use the phone to say something, send something, or tap to approve. Leave complex setup and troubleshooting for your computer.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it.

### Tip 1: Talk instead of typing {#tip1}

There are three ways to use your voice on the phone:

::: props
- Voice chat: With the composer empty, tap the waveform button, **Start voice chat**. The Bot answers out loud and keeps listening until you hang up
- Dictation: Tap **Start dictation** to turn what you say into a text message
- Voice memos: Play a **voice memo** the Bot sent you
:::

During a call you can **Mute**, **Hang up**, or choose **Show transcript** to read along. In a call with your own Bot, **Show settings** changes the voice, speed, and language, and **Auto-detect** picks the language from what you say. You can open other chats during a call, but only one voice chat runs at a time, and voice chat works with one Bot, not in group chats.

When you hang up, the chat shows a **Voice chat** card with the call's length. Open it to read the full transcript. If the call left work to do, the Bot may follow up in the chat. In version 1.18 and later of the phone app, a phone call, FaceTime, or another app taking over the audio ends the voice chat, and it doesn't resume on its own, so start a new one.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
We're on a voice chat and I'm walking, so listen first and sort it out after:
1. Turn what I say into [a to-do list], with a due date on each item.
2. Note anything you couldn't hear or that's ambiguous, and ask me about it in the chat after we hang up.
3. Don't contact anyone for me yet.
:::

### Tip 2: Send things from the system share sheet {#tip2}

In Photos, your browser, Files, or almost any app, open the system share sheet and choose **Grok Bot** to send something to a Bot without leaving the app:

| Platform | What you can share |
| --- | --- |
| iPhone | Photos, files, links, and text |
| Android | Text, photos, videos, audio, and PDFs |

If you have a primary Bot, it starts in **To:**. You can pick another Bot or search for one. Optionally choose **Add a note**, then **Send**. If the share can't be sent from there, choose **Open in Grok Bot** to finish in the app. As in the last issue, a one-line note on what it is and what to do with it beats a bare file.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
(A note to add when you share an article link)
This is an article I want to read properly later. Please:
1. Sum up its main points in three sentences.
2. List any figures it cites, with their sources.
3. Add it to my [reading list], and don't share it with anyone.
:::

### Tip 3: Let your Bot use your phone's apps {#tip3}

When you ask, a Bot can use some of the apps on your phone:

| App | What it can do | Platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calendar | Read events, and add, change, or delete them. It can't change repeating events and never invites anyone | iPhone, Android |
| Contacts | Look up a phone number, email, address, or birthday by name. Read only | iPhone, Android |
| Location | Read where your phone is right now, as a position rather than an address | iPhone, Android |
| Alarms | Set an alarm up to 24 hours ahead, or a timer of up to 24 hours | iPhone, Android |
| Reminders | Read, add, change, check off, or delete reminders | iPhone only |
| Home | Read and change Apple Home accessories such as lights, fans, blinds, thermostats, and locks | iPhone only |

Three things have to be true. It works only in your own chat with a Bot you own, not in group chats, Slack, or someone else's Team Bot. Grok Bot has to be open on the phone, or the Bot asks you to open it and try again. And most apps need your phone's permission first. To grant it ahead of time, open **Settings → Connect apps** and choose **iPhone** or **Android**. If you haven't, the chat shows a **Grok Bot needs permission** card the first time the Bot needs an app. The Bot waits up to five minutes, and if you allow the app within an hour, it can pick the request back up.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Look at my phone calendar for [tomorrow]:
1. Find any clashes, or meetings less than 15 minutes apart.
2. Set an alarm at [3 p.m.] to remind me to leave.
3. Only suggest changes. Don't edit or delete any events.
:::

### Tip 4: Start your Bot with one press on iPhone {#tip4}

Grok Bot adds shortcuts you can run from Siri, Spotlight, or the Shortcuts app: **List Bots**, **Send Message**, **Start Dictation**, and **Start Voice Chat**. For example, say "Send a message in Grok Bot."

::: steps
- Dictate without opening the app | Run **Start Dictation** while your iPhone is unlocked. A Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island shows progress, with buttons to cancel or send.
- Map the Action Button | In your iPhone's Settings, choose Action Button, swipe to Shortcut, and choose **Grok Bot** > **Start Voice Chat**. Press it to talk with a Bot, and press it again to hang up.
- Pick a Bot | If you don't pick a Bot for the shortcut, Grok Bot asks which one each time.
:::

To start a message to a specific Bot from another app or your own shortcut, use an official deep link. See Further reading at the end of this issue.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
From now on, when I call you with the Action Button, follow this routine:
1. Start by asking, "What should I note this time?"
2. When I'm done, repeat the key points back and wait for me to confirm.
3. Then write them up in our chat, list anything to do as to-dos, and wait for my approval before acting.
Repeat this routine back to me first.
:::

### Tip 5: Approve, take over, and manage routines from your phone {#tip5}

The most useful thing about the phone app is that you don't have to rush back to your desk when a Bot is waiting on you:

::: props
- Draft approvals: An email or Slack message the Bot prepared shows up as an editable draft. Choose **Send** when it's right, or **Discard**
- Computer takeover: Open the computer from a chat to watch the Bot work, step in for a password, two-factor code, or CAPTCHA, and hand control back with **I'm done**
- Routines: Open the Bot's profile to see each routine's schedule, next run, instruction, and **Run history**. Use **Active** to pause or resume, and **Edit routine** to change it
- Finding past work: Search from the home screen across Messages, Bots, Group Chats, Files, and Routines
- Notifications: Turn off **Notifications** for one Bot on its profile, or for every Bot in the phone app's Settings
:::

Note that you can't update or recover the cloud computer from your phone, and testing a routine or teaching by demonstration needs the desktop app.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I'm out for the rest of the day with only my phone. Please follow these rules:
1. If a step needs me to sign in or enter a code, tell me which site and which step, and I'll take over from my phone.
2. Prepare every email and message as a draft for me to review. Don't send anything directly.
3. Don't change any routine's schedule until I'm back at my computer.
Repeat the rules back to me first.
:::

## Limits you should know {#limits}

::: steps
- System versions | iPhone needs iOS 18 or later, iPad needs iPadOS 18 or later, and Android needs version 9 or later.
- Voice chat is one-to-one | You can't start one in a group chat, and only one voice chat runs at a time.
- Phone apps need the app open | When Grok Bot is closed or in the background, the Bot can't use your calendar, alarms, or other phone apps.
- Phone apps work in your own chat only | Group chats, Slack, and someone else's Team Bot can't use your phone's apps.
- Some things are desktop only | Updating or recovering the cloud computer, testing a routine, teaching by demonstration, and some advanced settings need the desktop app.
- Don't pay twice | If your plan already includes Grok Bot, sign in with the same account instead of buying another subscription through the App Store or Google Play.
:::

## Case study: how a fictional "Road Assistant" helps someone who travels a lot {#case}

Continuing our fictional cast (not anyone's real setup): a sales manager spends most days visiting clients with only her phone. She set up a Bot called "Road Assistant".

| Step | What she does | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notes on the go | Starts a voice chat with the Action Button and talks through the visit | Checks the transcript after hanging up |
| Quick forwarding | Shares a client's quote to the Bot from the share sheet | Adds a note on what to do with it |
| Scheduling | Has the Bot check her calendar and set a leave-now alarm | Suggestions only, no direct edits |
| Approvals | Reviews the follow-up email draft on her phone | Checks recipients before Send |
| Back at her desk | Handles anything that needs the desktop | Such as testing a new routine |

### Highlight: one call becomes a list of to-dos {#case-flow}

She talks for two minutes in a taxi, and by the time she gets out, a follow-up email draft is waiting for her.

::: flow
- Me | Talk through the visit | person
- Road Assistant | Writes up notes and to-dos
- Road Assistant | Drafts the follow-up email
- Me | Review the draft on my phone | person
- Client | Receives the follow-up | person
---
- Me → Road Assistant: Met the client today. They want a demo next week
- Road Assistant → Me: Three to-dos noted, and two possible demo times
- Me → Road Assistant: Go with Wednesday afternoon and draft a confirmation email
- Road Assistant → Me: The draft is ready for you to review
- Me → Client: The confirmation email is sent
:::

Later she had Road Assistant send her a list of the day's visits every morning, so one glance before leaving is enough.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You're this "Road Assistant". While I'm out, follow these rules:
1. After a voice chat, write up anything I asked for as to-dos in our chat.
2. For files I share with you, first say what you read, then follow my note.
3. For my calendar, suggest changes and wait for my OK. Prepare every email as a draft.
4. When you need me to take over the computer, say which step and why.
Repeat the rules back to me first.
:::

## Community picks: common ways to use it {#community}

Here are some common setups that are easy to start with. They're general ideas, so swap in your own details.

### A commute stand-up {#c1}

Start a voice chat on the way to work, have the Bot read out today's schedule and to-dos, and decide out loud what comes first.

### Snap a receipt, log the expense {#c2}

Photograph a receipt, share it to your Bot, and have it add the expense to your spreadsheet for a month-end summary.

### Save links for later {#c3}

When you find a good article in your browser, share it to your Bot with "read later" and have it summarize the piece and add it to your reading list.

### A leave-now alarm {#c4}

Have the Bot check when your next meeting starts and set an alarm for when you should leave.

### One-tap home scenes {#c5}

On iPhone, have the Bot turn off the living room lights and close the blinds in one go. For locks and garage doors, keep approvals on.
