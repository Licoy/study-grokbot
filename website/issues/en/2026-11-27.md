## What this issue covers {#intro}

Most recent issues have been about getting work done: sorting email, taking meeting notes, running errands. But your Bot can also make pictures and short clips: a header image for an article, a few seconds of motion from a product photo, or a short video that loops forever.

The "Files and results" page in the official docs has a section on creating images and videos. [Issue 13](/issues/2026-11-13/) only touched on it in a short paragraph. This issue covers the whole section in five tips, plus a few real projects from the community. For pages, the Library, and attachments, see Issue 13.

::: callout The one thing to remember
For images, describe the subject, style, and shape. For videos, pick a starting point first, then set the length and resolution. Send charts to a page, and don't ask a video for exact text.
:::

One thing up front: your Bot creates images and videos only when you ask. It sends each image and video it makes to the conversation, and images it creates still load in the conversation while its computer is asleep.

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the text in square brackets with your own and send it.

### Tip 1: Describe the picture in your own words, and pick a shape {#tip1}

The docs suggest describing what you want in your own words: the subject, the style, and the mood. Images can be square, wide, or tall, in five aspect ratios:

- **1:1**: square, good for avatars and square posts
- **4:3 and 3:4**: common landscape and portrait photo shapes
- **16:9**: widescreen, good for article headers and wide covers
- **9:16**: tall, good for full-screen phone views and video covers

Image creation isn't used for charts, plots, or tables. For those, ask your Bot for a [page](/issues/2026-11-13/), where numbers and text stay accurate.

::: prompt
Please create an image for [use, for example: the header of an article about weekend camping].
Subject: [for example: a tent by a lake with a lit lantern].
Style: [for example: warm film photography]. Mood: [for example: quiet, early evening].
Aspect ratio 16:9. Make one first, and I'll decide whether to change it.
:::

### Tip 2: Use your own photos as references {#tip2}

To build on pictures you already have, share them in the conversation and say how to use them. The Bot uses them as references.

::: steps
- Share | Send the reference images in the conversation, or paste them into the composer.
- Say how | Say what to keep and what to change, for example "keep the layout, make it a snowy scene."
- Try it on | The docs' own example: share a photo of yourself and ask to see an outfit on you.
- Real subjects | For a well-known person, product, or place, the Bot first looks for a real photo to use as a reference.
:::

Images can show real or invented people, including you. But if you only want to see what a real person, place, or product looks like, the Bot finds a real image instead of creating one. If it can't find one, it tells you rather than creating a picture.

::: prompt
Here's a photo I took of [subject, for example: my studio desk]. Please use it as a reference.
Keep [what to keep, for example: the desk layout and where the window is],
and change [what to change, for example: the daylight] to [new look, for example: warm lamp light at night].
Keep a shape close to the original, and make two versions for me to choose from.
:::

### Tip 3: Pick the video's starting point, then the specs {#tip3}

Ask for a video, an animation, or a clip. The docs list four starting points:

::: steps
- A description | Describe the scene in words, and the Bot makes it from scratch.
- One image | The image you share becomes the first frame.
- Two images | One for the start and one for the end. Use the same image for both to make a loop.
- Up to 14 reference images | Show how people, objects, clothing, or settings should look.
:::

You can also share up to 4 images that must appear at set moments in the clip. The specs:

::: props
- Length: 1 to 15 seconds, 6 by default
- Resolution: 480p unless you ask for 720p
- Aspect ratio: 1:1, 16:9, 9:16, 4:3, 3:4, 3:2, or 2:3. A clip made from your image keeps that image's shape unless you ask for another
- Inputs: images you share for a video must be JPEG, PNG, or WebP, and all the images for one clip can add up to about 35 MB
- File: each clip is saved as an MP4 file
- Time: creating a video can take a few minutes
:::

::: prompt
Make a looping clip from the image I shared: use it as both the first and the last frame.
Let [what moves, for example: the rain outside the window and the steam from the cup] move gently, and keep everything else still.
Aspect ratio 9:16, 8 seconds, 720p.
:::

### Tip 4: Choose subjects that suit video {#tip4}

According to the docs, videos look filmed. That makes some subjects a great fit and others better done another way.

::: props
- Good fit: a live scene, a still photo brought to life, or a character from your reference images
- Not a fit: motion graphics, title cards, slideshows, product or app demos, and charts
- Also not a fit: anything that needs exact text, timing, or brand assets
:::

When what you want falls on the "not a fit" side, change the approach: have the Bot put data and charts on a page, and for exact captions or brand elements, create clean footage first and handle the text and logo separately. One more detail: when the Bot hands work to a task, that task can create images and videos too, and the Bot then sends them to the conversation.

::: prompt
I want to make [what, for example: a short clip announcing our new shop opening].
First, tell me whether this suits generated video.
For the parts that do, make a few realistic shots.
For parts that need exact text, dates, or our logo, tell me how you'd handle them, and don't generate those yet.
:::

### Tip 5: View, save, and find what your Bot made {#tip5}

Images and videos appear in the conversation, and you can find them again in that Bot's Library.

::: steps
- View on desktop | Click an image, or a video's thumbnail, to open it full screen, then click the image to zoom in. With more than one item, use the left and right arrow keys or the thumbnails. Close with Escape, the close button, or a click outside.
- Save on desktop | Click the download button at the top of the viewer. Or right-click an image for **Copy image** or **Save image…**, or right-click a video for **Save video…**.
- Download in bulk | Hover over a message, click the three dots, and choose **Download**, or **Download all** when the message has several items.
- Player | Videos have play and pause, a seek bar, and a speed menu with 2×, 1.5×, 1×, and 0.5×.
- On the phone | Tap an image to open it full screen, then choose **Forward** to send it to another chat, **Share**, or **Save**. Touch and hold an image for **Copy Image** and **Save to Photos**, or a video for **Save to Photos**.
:::

To find older pictures, open the Bot's details and go to the Library tab, or press `Cmd/Ctrl+K` on desktop to search. See [Issue 26](/issues/2026-11-26/).

::: prompt
Please sort out the images and videos you made for me this week.
Group them by purpose. For each one, list the file name, the aspect ratio, the length (for videos), and my original request,
and mark which versions I ended up using.
:::

## Boundaries you should know {#limits}

::: props
- Only on request: your Bot doesn't create images or videos unless you ask
- Real subjects get real images: to show what a real person, place, or product looks like, the Bot finds a real image, and if it can't, it tells you instead of creating one
- Content rules: some requests are blocked, and the Bot tells you. Naming trademarked characters, brands, or real people often causes a block, so describe the subject in your own words and try again
- Busy: if image creation is busy, the Bot may ask you to try again in a moment
- Turned off: if the Bot says image or video generation isn't available for your account, creation is turned off for you. On a team, an admin's model settings or the team's privacy settings can cause this, so ask your admin
- No charts: for charts, plots, and tables, ask for a page
:::

## Case study: a fictional florist makes a week of post images in one afternoon {#case}

A fictional scenario (not anyone's real setup): a florist posts a few times a week on social media and used to shoot and edit every photo herself. This week she hands the pictures and a short clip to her primary Bot.

::: props
- Starting point: a few photos of bouquets taken in the shop
- Goal: three square images and one tall looping clip
- Concern: the shop name and prices must be exact, and she posts everything herself
:::

### Highlights: photos as references, the same first and last frame, and text added by hand {#case-flow}

::: steps
- Images | Following Tip 2, she shares the bouquet photos as references and asks for three 1:1 images that keep the bouquets and change only the background.
- Looping clip | Following Tip 3, she uses the best photo as both the first and last frame for a 9:16, 6-second loop where the petals sway gently.
- No text in frame | Following Tip 4, she keeps the shop name and prices out of the pictures and writes them in the post caption herself.
- Wrap-up | Following Tip 5, she uses **Download all** in the conversation, and next week she finds them again in the Library to make changes.
:::

::: prompt
These are photos of bouquets from my shop. Please use them as references.
1. Make three 1:1 images that keep the bouquets and change the background to [background one], [background two], and [background three].
2. Use [one of the photos] as both the first and last frame for a 9:16, 6-second looping clip where only the petals sway gently.
3. Don't put any text, shop name, or prices in the pictures.
Send them to me to choose from, and don't post them anywhere.
:::

## Community picks: what people have made with their Bots {#community}

This issue gathers real projects that people shared on X, plus one tip based on the official docs.

### One line of a play, one music video {#c1}

::: quote @Frank_SI_Lab | 2026-10-14 | https://x.com/Frank_SI_Lab/status/2110285369186844888
I gave Grok Bot one line of Shakespeare. It came back with a full, animated music video. Grok Bot composed the song (with ElevenLabs Music). Grok Bot created every image (in Canva). Grok Bot brought them to life (with Grok Imagine). Grok Bot cut the video.
:::

The lesson is in the division of labor: the author handed the music, images, animation, and editing to the Bot and supplied only a single line to start from.

### Design one character, then build 15 scenes {#c2}

::: quote @luketestsai | 2026-10-20 | https://x.com/luketestsai/status/2112388486296666231
Grok Bot designed one character. Grok Bot generated 15 scenes from that one reference image, timed to the song. Cut the glitchy shots. Close-ups kept giving him an extra leg or a waterfall coming off his chin, so a couple became landscape shots instead.
:::

This is the same idea as the reference images in Tip 3: settle on one character image first and use it as the reference for every scene, so the character stays consistent.

### Let your Bot weed out bad takes {#c3}

::: quote @MikaMilaniX | 2026-10-20 | https://x.com/MikaMilaniX/status/2112270344556052759
Me: Why did you post a photo? I asked for a video. Grok Bot: I tried, but all three videos failed. Me: What was wrong with them? Grok Bot: Extra limbs, melting hands, warped pearls.
:::

You can say this in the prompt up front: check the clips yourself and don't hand over ones with obvious flaws, but tell me how many tries you made and what went wrong, and show me if I ask.

### Stick-figure animation, drawn with code {#c4}

::: quote @nhaskins | 2026-11-18 | https://x.com/nhaskins/status/2122812028402159861
My Grok Bot video agent wrote a Python rig and drew every frame with OpenCV. The full 3-minute song (4,340 frames) rendered in about 3 minutes across 8 parallel workers. No image model.
:::

The docs say generated video isn't made for things like motion graphics, so this author took another route: have the Bot write code that draws each frame. For line animation, or anything that needs precise control, this approach is worth a try.

### Same first and last frame, a seamless loop {#c5}

The docs say that using the same image for the start and the end makes a loop, and a clip can be 9:16, which fits a tall animated cover or background on a phone. Community members have shared this tip too.
