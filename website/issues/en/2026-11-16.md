## What this issue covers {#intro}

The last dozen or so issues were about putting your Bot to work. But anyone who uses it every day has hit moments like these: the Bot goes quiet, the computer is stuck on Reconnecting, yesterday's routine didn't run, or a Bot is missing from the sidebar. The easiest mistake to make in that moment is to panic and choose Reset.

The official docs have a page called "Troubleshooting", and its first line is: start with the least destructive step. This issue turns that page into five tips: try the lightest checks first, recover the computer in order, find chats and files that seem to be gone, deal with stuck Bots and routines that didn't run, and fix websites and apps that won't connect. Then we cover how to contact support effectively.

::: callout The one thing to remember
Quit and reopen first, check the status page first, and let the Bot tell you where it's stuck. Reset is always the last step, and a computer problem doesn't delete your chats.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it.

### Tip 1: Start with the lightest step, then check the status page {#tip1}

Most problems clear with a full restart: fully quit Grok Bot (on Mac, choose **Quit** from the menu bar, not just closing the window), reopen it, and try again. A Bot's cloud work can continue while the app is disconnected.

::: props
- Is the service down: Open status.cursor.com and look at the **Grok Bot** row. The app doesn't show service status. If there's an incident, wait for the fix, then send your message again. Don't Reset the computer or reinstall the app
- No reply for now: If there's no incident but the Bot says **Bot failed to respond** or **Model provider is overloaded**, wait a moment and send it again. If it still doesn't reply, fully quit and reopen
- Your device is offline: **You're offline** means your device has no internet connection. Your Bots keep working in the cloud, and the open chat can show **Showing saved messages**
- Usage used up: A Bot that says you hit your usage limit isn't broken. Weekly usage resets each week. To keep going now, add credit or move to a bigger plan
- App too old: When your version is more than 14 days old and a newer one is out, the app shows **Update required**. Choose **Update**, then **Restart to update**. Your Bots keep running in the cloud the whole time
:::

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Something seems wrong on my side. Please run a quick self-check:
1. Tell me whether you can reach your computer right now.
2. List the last task you didn't finish and the step where it stopped.
3. If you need me to do something (sign in, approve, update), tell me which step.
Only report back. Don't retry any task yet.
:::

### Tip 2: When the computer can't be reached, recover in order and save Reset for last {#tip2}

When the computer is temporarily unreachable, your Bot profiles and saved conversations aren't lost. Chats are stored separately from the computer, and Bots, files, and logins are safe while it reconnects. If the computer's card says **Unreachable** while chat still works, a VPN or company firewall may be blocking the connection, so try another network.

::: steps
- Wait and retry | If the computer says **Reconnecting** or **Couldn't Reach Grok Bot's Computer**, wait, then choose **Retry**. Don't Reset during this.
- Fully quit and reopen | Quit Grok Bot completely, then open it again.
- Update if offered | Open **Settings → Computer** and choose **Update Bot's Computer**.
- Then try Recover | If it still can't reconnect, or an update looks stuck and **Recover computer** is shown, choose it.
- Reset last | Use **Reset** only after those steps. On the phone, **Settings → Bot → Bot Computer** has **Reset Computer**. Updating and recovering need the desktop app.
:::

Each option keeps and removes different things:

| Action | Keeps | Removes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software update | Everything, including installed apps | Nothing |
| Full computer update | Bots, files, and logins | Installed apps and packages |
| Recover computer | Saved Bots, files, and logins | Installed apps and packages. Running work is interrupted, and very recent changes can be lost if the computer couldn't be reached |
| Reset | Your last saved data | The same as Recover |

Project folders such as `node_modules` or a Python virtual environment aren't saved, so reinstall them after an update, Recover, or Reset. Don't start a second Update or Reset while one is running. If Recover or Reset fails, choose **Retry Recovery** or **Retry Reset** once, then stop and contact support. If Bots recently disappeared or saving failed, contact support before you use Recover or Reset.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I'm about to update your computer. First, take stock for me:
1. List the apps and packages we installed on the computer, with the commands used to install them.
2. List the project folders whose dependencies will need reinstalling.
3. Save that list as a file on the computer, and use it to help me restore everything after the update.
:::

### Tip 3: When chats, Bots, or files seem gone, don't touch anything yet {#tip3}

They are usually not deleted. The docs are clear: don't use **Recover** or **Reset** to look for them. Check in this order:

::: steps
- Fully quit and reopen | Quit Grok Bot completely, then open it again.
- Try another device | Open the same account on your phone or another computer. Bots and conversations are shared across devices signed in to that account.
- Look for hidden Bots | On desktop, open **Hidden Bots** in the sidebar. On the phone, open **Show Hidden Bots**. **Hide from sidebar** only removes a Bot from the list. Hidden Bots stay active and keep their history. Choose **Show in sidebar** on desktop or **Unhide** on the phone to bring one back.
- Confirm the account | Make sure you're in the same Cursor account, not a different login.
:::

As for files: deleting files inside the computer, even with `rm -rf`, doesn't delete chats. Grok Bot keeps a durable copy of synced computer data and restores from it when the computer reopens, so synced files usually come back. If the computer is still empty after **Recover**, stop making changes inside it and contact support. Files that exist only on your own Mac or Windows machine aren't covered, so keep your own backups. When the computer is almost full, your Bot tells you once in chat, names the biggest files or folders, and deletes nothing unless you say yes. Low disk space isn't data loss.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
I can't find [a Bot] in my sidebar. Please check for me:
1. Whether you can see a list of all the Bots on my account, and whether it's there.
2. Whether it's hidden, and if so, where I can bring it back.
3. Don't recover, reset, or delete anything until I confirm.
:::

### Tip 4: Stuck Bots, expired approvals, and routines that didn't run {#tip4}

When a Bot looks stuck, it's usually waiting for you:

::: props
- Check the status: Look at the status in the sidebar and the conversation. **Needs attention** means a question, approval, or handoff is waiting for you
- Check the screen: Open the computer to see whether the Bot is waiting on a page
- Find the request: Look for a question, approval, login, CAPTCHA, or secret request
- Redirect: If the approach is wrong, send a short correction
- Stop: If the work should end, send a direct "Stop now"
:::

Expired approvals are common too. An approval from a chat you're in waits for you. An approval from work that started without you, like a routine, a trigger, or a message from another Bot, shows **Expired** after about 30 minutes, and the action doesn't run. Turn on **Notifications** for Bots that run routines, choose **Always allow** for actions you trust, and ask the Bot to try again after one expires. If an action keeps asking for approval, check **Custom Rules** under **Settings → General → Bot** for a matching **Ask first** rule, including team rules your admin requires. **Ask first** wins over **Allow automatically**.

When a routine didn't run, open it and check each item:

::: steps
- Enabled | It isn't **Paused**.
- Right time | **When to run** shows the schedule and time zone you expect.
- Bot still there | The Bot that owns it still exists.
- Apps still connected | The apps it needs are still signed in, and the computer can reach the source system.
- Usage and account | Usage or account access hasn't been paused.
- Run history | Look for a failure in recent runs. A **Test** run can perform real external actions, so use it only with safe input.
:::

If a routine isn't paused but hasn't run for 24 hours or more with no error shown, report a bug with the Bot name and the routine details.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
My [routine] didn't run on time this morning. Please check each of these:
1. Whether it's enabled, and its next run time and time zone.
2. Whether every app it uses is still connected.
3. Whether any recent runs failed, and why.
Only report back. Don't run it by hand, because it might send messages to other people.
:::

### Tip 5: Website sign-ins, connection errors, and app sign-in {#tip5}

If a website keeps asking you to sign in, take over once: choose **Take over** on the **Computer** card, sign in and complete two-factor authentication or the CAPTCHA, make sure the signed-in page has loaded, then choose **I'm done** and tell the Bot to continue from the current page. Don't paste a password or code into ordinary chat. Fingerprint, Face ID, Windows Hello, and passkeys saved on your laptop or phone don't work on the Bot's computer.

::: props
- A site times out or won't connect: The Bot's computer reaches the internet through Cursor's shared network, and some sites drop connections from it. Update, Recover, and Reset don't change this. Under **Settings → Computer → Your computer**, turn on **Route traffic through this computer** so the Bot's browser uses your desktop's connection
- An app is added but doesn't work: **Added** means installed, not signed in. Open **Connect apps** in the sidebar. **Needs auth** or **Disconnected** means choose **Authenticate**. **Disabled by team admin** means ask your team admin
- Sign-in is stuck: If the connect card is waiting for authorization, choose **Reopen**. If authorization didn't finish or failed, choose **Retry**. If the button changes to **Reconnect**, choose it
- Still not working: Choose **Remove**, add the app again, and sign in
:::

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You couldn't open [a website] just now. Don't keep retrying. Work through this in order:
1. Tell me whether it's asking for a sign-in, or whether it's a timeout or connection error.
2. If it needs a sign-in, send me a takeover request and tell me which step it's on, and I'll do it.
3. If it's a connection problem, tell me the exact error, and I'll decide whether to route traffic through my computer.
:::

## Before you contact support {#support}

On desktop, open the account menu and choose **Support → Send Feedback**. If the problem is in the open conversation, keep **Include current conversation ID** selected. In the phone app, open **Settings** and tap **Send Feedback**. If the problem is with one Bot, choose it and keep **Include Bot ID** on. For help from the support team, email support@x.ai from the email on your Grok Bot account.

::: steps
- Version info | In the account menu, choose **Support → About**, then **Copy version info**. It copies the version, release track, and OS.
- Request ID | Right-click the failed message and choose **Copy request ID**, or hover it and open **More message actions**. If there's no such option, include the error text and a screenshot instead.
- What happened | What you were trying to do, the Bot or routine name, the exact error or a screenshot, and the approximate time and time zone.
- What you tried | For example, retrying, restarting the app, or updating the computer.
- Never include | Passwords, one-time codes, private keys, or any secret values.
:::

## Limits you should know {#limits}

::: steps
- Reset comes last | Reset doesn't fix outages, sites that won't connect, or missing chats, and it can lose very recent changes.
- Reinstall project dependencies | After an update, Recover, or Reset, reinstall `node_modules` and Python virtual environments.
- Back up your own files | Files that exist only on your own computer aren't covered.
- Reinstalling rarely helps | For a sign-in loop, reinstalling the app usually doesn't fix it. Update the app and clear your browser cache first.
- Some problems need support | Some session and account-link problems can't be fixed on your side. Contact support with the exact error and the steps you tried.
:::

## Case study: a fictional "Morning Brief" Bot misses a morning {#case}

Continuing our fictional cast (not anyone's real setup): an indie developer has a Bot called "Morning Brief". Every morning a routine rounds up the previous day's product feedback into a short brief. One morning, the brief doesn't show up.

| Step | What happened | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Status page | No incident on the Grok Bot row | Not a service problem, so no reinstall and no Reset |
| Sidebar | Morning Brief shows Needs attention | An approval card was waiting |
| Approval card | It already says Expired; nobody approved it overnight | The expired action didn't run |
| Routine | Enabled, with the right time and time zone | The schedule itself was fine |
| Fix | Ask Morning Brief to redo it and turn on its Notifications | Choose Always allow for read-only actions you trust |

### Highlight: the cause found in ten minutes, without touching Reset {#case-flow}

From noticing the missing brief to getting it out, everything was just checking and chatting. The computer was never touched.

::: flow
- Me | Notice the missing brief | person
- Morning Brief | Self-check and explain the blocker
- Me | Approve and turn on notifications | person
- Morning Brief | Produce the brief again
---
- Me → Morning Brief: Today's brief didn't arrive. Don't retry yet, tell me where it got stuck
- Morning Brief → Me: The overnight run needed one approval, nobody answered it, and it expired
- Me → Morning Brief: Allow this kind of read-only action from now on, and redo it now
- Morning Brief → Me: The brief is ready. I'll notify you next time I need you
:::

Later he asked Morning Brief to run a self-check every Sunday evening, listing every expired approval and failed run that week, so he could decide which rules to adjust.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You are "Morning Brief". When something goes wrong, follow these rules:
1. When a run fails or an approval expires, tell me why first. Don't keep retrying on your own.
2. When you need me to sign in, approve, or take over, tell me which website and which step.
3. Never recover or reset the computer yourself. Ask me first if you think it's needed.
4. Every Sunday evening, sum up this week's failures and expired approvals.
Repeat the rules back to me first.
:::

## Community picks: common ways to use it {#community}

Here are a few common, easy-to-start ideas for reference. They're general patterns, so swap in your own details.

### Subscribe to the status page {#c1}

Subscribe to updates on status.cursor.com. When the service has a problem, you hear about it first instead of chasing it as your own issue.

### Take stock before updating {#c2}

Before each computer update, ask your Bot to list the apps and packages it installed, then reinstall them from that list afterwards.

### One-line self-check {#c3}

Save the prompt from Tip 1 as a snippet. When a Bot has been quiet for too long, send it so it tells you where it's stuck.

### No overnight approvals {#c4}

Turn on notifications for Bots that run routines, and set read-only actions you trust to allow automatically, so overnight jobs don't stall on expired approvals.

### A support template {#c5}

Keep a template for contacting support: version info, request ID, time and time zone, and the steps you tried. Fill it in when something breaks.
