## What this issue covers {#intro}

The tiring part of a meeting is often not the discussion. It's taking notes while you listen, writing them up afterwards, and handing out the action items one by one. Grok Bot's Meetings feature takes that on: a Bot can join Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams calls, listen, take notes, answer questions in the meeting chat, and send you a summary and action items when the call ends.

According to the docs, Meetings are available on Team, Enterprise, and Ultra plans, and Enterprise admins turn them on first. This issue starts with adding a Bot to a call and ends with putting its notes to work.

::: callout The one thing to remember
Send the Bot a meeting link, approve the card, and have the host admit it. It listens and takes notes, and when the call ends the summary and action items show up in its chat.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it.

### Tip 1: Add a Bot to a meeting in three steps {#tip1}

The documented flow is short:

::: steps
- Paste the link | Paste the Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams link into your chat with the Bot and ask it to join and take notes.
- Approve the card | The Bot asks for approval with a card. Approve it.
- The host admits it | When the meeting starts, the host lets the Bot in. It joins as "Bot name (your first name's Bot) - transcribing", or "Bot name (Team Bot) - transcribing" for a Team Bot.
:::

A Bot that follows your calendar can also join scheduled meetings on its own. Not sure what to ask? Open the Bot's details. If it hasn't joined a meeting before, **Add to meeting** under **Suggested** drafts a question asking the Bot how to add it to meetings.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Here's the link for my meeting tomorrow: [paste meeting link]. It starts at [date and time].
Please join and take notes:
1. Only take notes. Don't speak up in the meeting on your own.
2. After the call, send me a summary that lists decisions and action items (who, what, by when).
3. If someone in the meeting chat asks you for material, tell me who asked first. Don't send it.
Show me a card for anything I need to approve.
:::

### Tip 2: Manage meetings under Upcoming {#tip2}

Open the Bot's details. Meetings it's joining or attending appear under **Upcoming**, with a countdown to the join time, the next join time for a scheduled meeting, or **Taking notes** while it's in the call.

Hover a meeting you scheduled and open its menu to see Meeting actions:

| Action | What it does | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disable Interactivity | Keeps listening and taking notes, without answering in the meeting | The row shows Muted. While the meeting is still scheduled, Enable Interactivity turns it back on |
| Cancel | Removes a scheduled meeting | Use it when a meeting moves or is called off |
| Quit | Takes the Bot out of a meeting it's joining or attending | Once it's joining or in the call, quit and add it again to turn interactivity back on |

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Please list the meetings you're set to join. Report only, don't change anything:
1. List each meeting's name and join time in order.
2. Mark which ones are recurring.
3. Point out any you might not need to attend anymore (for example, titles that say "cancelled" or "moved").
I'll decide what to cancel under Upcoming myself.
:::

### Tip 3: Ask by name in the meeting chat {#tip3}

During a call, address the Bot by name in the meeting chat and it answers there. The docs also cover a few special cases:

::: props
- Zoom private chat: A private chat message to the Bot gets a private answer
- Conference rooms: A request from a conference room device counts as coming from the room, not one person
- Held-back requests: If someone asks for something the Bot held back, it tells you who asked and offers to send it
- Credentials: It won't share your credentials in a meeting
:::

A handy use during a call is a quick recap, such as "What did we decide about the budget?". It's much faster than digging through notes later.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
People may ask you questions by name in today's meeting chat. Let's agree the rules:
1. Only answer questions about this meeting, in three sentences or fewer.
2. For anything from my files, email, or accounts, ask me privately first. Don't post it in the meeting chat.
3. If someone asks you to send something or do something, note who asked and what for, and tell me after the call.
Repeat these rules back to me.
:::

### Tip 4: Turn the notes into action after the call {#tip4}

When the call ends, the Bot sends a **summary and action items** in its chat. If you have Pages, a longer report goes on a page, with the link in the chat. You can ask the Bot anytime for the summary or transcript of a past meeting.

One more thing: meetings use the settings of **the person who started them**.

The notes are only the start. It saves more time to have the Bot put the action items into the shape you need, while sends and new tasks still wait for your OK.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Using your notes from the [meeting name] we just had, please do three things:
1. Put the action items in a table: owner, task, due date. Mark "TBD" where no date was mentioned.
2. Draft a follow-up email to the attendees with only the decisions and action items. Show it to me first, don't send it.
3. List questions that came up without an answer, so we can pick them up next time.
:::

### Tip 5: For Team Bots, check the team switch first {#tip5}

Team Bots can join meetings too, when their team allows it. On Enterprise plans, admins control meetings for everyone's Bots with the **Meetings** switch under **Bot Capabilities** on the Grok Bot page of the team dashboard:

::: steps
- Switch on | Members' Bots can join Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams calls, transcribe them, and report back.
- Switch off | Bots can't join or read meetings. A Bot that was scheduled to join tells you meetings are no longer available.
- Team Bots | A Team Bot joins meetings only when its own team allows them. It joins as "Bot name (Team Bot) - transcribing".
:::

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
Our team wants the shared [Team Bot name] to join our weekly meeting. Please draft a note for our admin. Draft only, don't send it:
1. Say which switch needs to be on in the team dashboard.
2. Explain what the Bot does in a meeting (listens, takes notes, sends a summary and action items afterwards).
3. Say how we plan to use it: notes only, no speaking in the meeting.
:::

## Limits you should know {#limits}

::: steps
- Plans | Meetings are available on Team, Enterprise, and Ultra plans. On Enterprise, an admin turns them on first.
- The host admits it | The Bot doesn't walk into a call by itself. The host has to let it in.
- Settings follow the starter | Meetings use the settings of the person who started them.
- Mute early | Once the Bot is joining or in the call, the only way to turn interactivity back on is to quit and add it again.
- No credentials | The Bot won't share your credentials in a meeting, and it tells you about requests it held back.
- Respect attendees | Before the Bot takes notes, it's good practice to let other attendees know a Bot is transcribing.
:::

## Case study: how a fictional "Minute Taker" handles weekly meetings {#case}

Continuing our fictional cast (not anyone's real setup): a small-team lead has three standing meetings a week. She used to take notes during each one and spend half an hour writing them up. Now she hands that job to a Bot called "Minute Taker".

| Situation | What she does | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday stand-up | Pastes the link and asks for notes only | Uses Disable Interactivity to keep it quiet |
| Client call | Lets it answer agenda questions in the chat | Internal material goes through her first |
| Ad hoc discussion | Asks "What did we just decide?" during the call | Answers stay within three sentences |
| After the call | Gets the summary and action items, then asks for an email draft | She checks the email before it goes out |
| Meeting moved | Uses Cancel under Upcoming | Pastes the new link when it's rescheduled |

### Highlight: one link in, action items out {#case-flow}

She only does two things: paste the link before the call and read the result after it.

::: flow
- Me | Paste the link and agree the rules | person
- Minute Taker | Joins once the host admits it
- Minute Taker | Notes decisions and action items
- Minute Taker | Sends a summary and action items after the call
- Me | Review and hand out tasks | person
---
- Me → Minute Taker: Notes only, and tell me before sending anyone material
- Minute Taker → Me: The meeting is under Upcoming
- Minute Taker → Me: Call ended. Three decisions, five action items
- Me → Minute Taker: Put the action items in a table, then draft the email
- Minute Taker → Me: Draft ready, waiting for your OK before sending
:::

Once, someone in the meeting chat asked Minute Taker to "send last week's quote". It didn't post it. After the call it told her who had asked and asked whether to send it.

::: prompt Example prompt · copy and send to your Bot
You're this "Minute Taker". When I send you a meeting link, follow these rules:
1. Take notes only and don't speak in the meeting, unless I say you can.
2. After the call, give me a one-line takeaway first, then the decisions and an action-item table.
3. If anyone in the meeting asks you for anything, note who and what, and ask me after the call.
4. Draft any email or task for me to check before it goes out.
Repeat the rules back to me first.
:::

## Community picks: common ways to use it {#community}

Here are some common setups that are easy to start with. They're general ideas, so swap in your own details.

### Automatic notes for recurring meetings {#c1}

Paste the link for a recurring meeting once, and every call ends with a summary and action items. No one has to be the note-taker.

### A silent observer {#c2}

Use Disable Interactivity to keep the Bot quietly taking notes. It suits formal meetings where an extra voice isn't welcome.

### Quick recaps during the call {#c3}

Joined late or lost the thread? Ask the Bot by name in the meeting chat, "Where are we?", and catch up in a few lines.

### Action items straight into your list {#c4}

After the call, have the Bot put the action items into a task or notes app you've connected, and check them before they land.

### Look back across meetings {#c5}

Can't remember a decision from a few days ago? Ask the Bot for that meeting's summary or transcript.
