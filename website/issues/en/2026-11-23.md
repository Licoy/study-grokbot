## What this issue covers {#intro}

Earlier issues covered how a Bot uses its computer and connected apps, and [Issue 15](/issues/2026-11-15/) covered how cards and wallets in Vault pay for things. This issue takes a different angle: treat your Bot as an assistant who runs errands, and see how far it can go with three everyday chores: picking things to buy, making phone calls, and sending messages.

These chores share one thing: the last step spends money, speaks to someone outside, or goes out in your name. The official design puts a card in front of you at that step, and you decide after checking it. The five tips below show you how to hand off the work while reading each card and keeping that last step in your hands.

::: callout The one thing to remember
Let the Bot pick, compare, dial, and draft. The final tap to pay, call, or send is yours, after you've read the card.
:::

## Five tips you can use today {#tips}

Each tip comes with an example prompt. Replace the parts in square brackets with your own details and send it. Payment and sign-in steps must be done by you.

### Tip 1: Let your Bot pick with product cards {#tip1}

The official docs say that when you ask a Bot to find something to buy, it can reply with product cards. Open one to see:

::: props
- Photos and options: product images and choices such as size or colour
- Why it fits: the Bot's reason it matches what you asked for
- Where to buy: where you can get it, and you can buy it from there
- Buy now or Out of stock: on desktop, each card also has Buy now, or says Out of stock
:::

The more specific you are, the more useful the cards: give a budget, the use, what it must have, and what you don't want, then ask for a comparison.

::: prompt
Help me pick a [type of product] for [specific use].
Budget up to [amount]. It must have [requirement one] and [requirement two]; no [feature you don't want].
Give me three options as product cards, and use Why it fits to explain the trade-offs.
Don't place an order yet.
:::

### Tip 2: Let your Bot watch for a restock {#tip2}

If the one you want is sold out, you don't have to keep checking. The official docs say: open the sold-out product, choose **Notify me**, and the Bot sets up a routine that tells you when it's back in stock.

That routine works like any routine you create: you can pause or delete it. See [Issue 2](/issues/2026-11-02/) for managing routines. Once you've bought it or changed your mind, ask the Bot to stop it.

::: prompt
[Product name] is sold out. Please watch for a restock.
When it's back, tell me, along with the price and the options available then.
Only notify me; don't order it for me.
I'll tell you once I've bought it, and then please stop this reminder.
:::

### Tip 3: Check every line of the order card before you pay {#tip3}

When you're ready to buy, the Bot sends an order card. The official docs list what's on it. Check each line before paying:

::: steps
- Store and items | Which store, which items, and the right options.
- Ship to | The address it will go to.
- Pay with | The card or wallet it will charge.
- Total | Whether the total fits your budget.
- Gift | For a gift, an extra row shows who it's for, your message, whether a gift receipt is included, and when it arrives.
:::

If it all looks right, choose the pay button, which shows the total. To back out, choose **Cancel**, and nothing is charged. After the order is placed, the card shows what you **Paid with**. If the order card expires, ask the Bot to check the price again. Only a Bot's owner can pay its orders. For setting up cards and wallets, see [Issue 15](/issues/2026-11-15/).

::: prompt
Let's buy [product name] in [option].
Ship it to my usual address and use my default payment method.
Before you send the order card, tell me in one sentence what the total includes (item, shipping, tax).
If the total is over [amount], don't send the order card; just tell me.
:::

### Tip 4: In the US, let your Bot make the phone call {#tip4}

The official docs say a Bot can call a business for you, for example to book a table or ask about an order, and tell you how it went. Phone calls are available in the US only, from the Grok Bot desktop and phone apps.

::: steps
- Agree to the terms | The first time, the Bot asks you to agree to the calling terms, including that you'll only call US businesses and people who've consented to hear from you. Choose Accept or Decline.
- Confirm before dialing | Before each call, the Bot shows who it will call. Choose Call or Cancel.
- During the call | The card shows the status and a live transcript. Choose End call to hang up.
- Take over | When Take over shows, choose it to join the call yourself. The card says Connecting you… and then Transferred to you.
- Answer a question | If the other side asks something the Bot can't answer, a card shows the question and how long you have. Type in Your answer and choose Send, or choose Skip.
:::

If the Bot wants to give the call more details while it's live, it asks you first. When the call is over, the card says Ended with the call's length. Open it to read the transcript and play the recording; on desktop you can also copy or export the transcript and download the recording. The docs also say these calls aren't used to train models, whatever your privacy settings.

Before the call, give the task, what you'll accept, and your limits in one message, so the Bot doesn't have to improvise on the line.

::: prompt
Please call [business name] at [number] to [task, for example book a table for two at 7 pm on Saturday].
I can accept: [for example any time between 6:30 and 8:00].
Don't agree to any extra charges or memberships. If they ask for my personal details, ask me first.
Afterward, sum it up in three lines: whether it worked, the confirmed time and name, and anything I still need to do.
:::

### Tip 5: Review drafts first, and redirect work at any time {#tip5}

When a Bot writes an email or a Slack message for you, the chat shows an editable draft card marked **Needs approval**. Check the recipients and body, then choose **Send** (or **Send message** for a Slack draft in the desktop app). Choose **Discard** to drop it. A Gmail or Outlook draft can include files the Bot prepared; if a file changed after the draft was prepared, nothing is sent and the card opens again so you can review it.

If you trust a Bot, open the menu on one of its drafts and choose **Disable drafts for Bot**. It then sends directly, still subject to Auto Review. The choice applies to that Bot only, only its owner sees it, and it isn't offered in group chats. To get drafts back, choose Needs approval on a draft card or ask the Bot.

If you change your mind halfway through, you don't have to wait:

::: steps
- Change priorities | Send a new message. A direct message from you takes priority over background work and can redirect the current turn.
- Stop at once | Send a clear "Stop now". It does not undo actions already completed.
- Point at one result | Use Reply on the result or approval request, so the context stays with it.
- Don't rely on reactions | Reactions are fine for a light acknowledgement; write out any decision that matters for safety.
:::

See [Issue 5](/issues/2026-11-05/) for approvals and Auto Review.

::: prompt
Write an email to [recipient] about [topic].
Keep it [formal or casual], under [number] words, and attach [file name].
Only prepare a draft; don't send it. In the draft, list any dates and amounts I need to confirm separately.
:::

## When to stop and let you decide {#boundaries}

The official docs suggest setting boundaries in the request itself, especially for sending messages or invitations, publishing content, purchases and financial transfers, deleting or overwriting data, changing permissions, production changes, and accepting legal terms. Errands mostly fall into the first three.

::: steps
- Paying | A card saved in Vault fills in at checkout without asking each time. To approve every payment yourself, don't save the card; see Issue 15.
- Dialing | Every call needs your Call. If they ask for identity checks or details you'd rather not hand over, Take over yourself.
- Sending | Keep drafts on. Email from the Bot's own address that you didn't request in chat, such as from a routine, always waits as a draft.
- Done is done | An approval controls the proposed action. Saying stop doesn't reverse work already completed.
:::

::: prompt
From now on, when you run errands for me:
1. Send an order card for any payment, and ask me first if the total is over [amount].
2. Before a phone call, tell me what you'll say, and never agree to extra charges for me.
3. Any email or message in my name is a draft first.
Repeat these rules back to me first.
:::

## Example: a made-up office worker clears three errands on a Friday {#case}

A made-up scenario (not anyone's real setup): an office worker in the US wants to hand off three errands on Friday afternoon: pick a birthday gift for a friend, book dinner for Saturday, and reply to an email from the landlord.

::: props
- Gift: the Bot shows three product cards within budget, then an order card with the Gift row filled in
- Dinner: the Bot explains what it will say, dials after confirmation, and a question card pops up when the restaurant asks about allergies
- Email: the Bot drafts the reply, keeps it as a draft, and flags the dates to confirm
:::

### Highlight: the Bot runs the errands, the person taps three times {#case-flow}

All afternoon, the Bot does the picking, dialing, and drafting. The three taps to pay, call, and send are the person's, after reading each card.

::: flow
- Owner | Hands off the tasks and decides on each card | person
- Main Bot | Picks the gift, makes the call, writes the draft
- Restaurant | Answers the phone
---
- Owner → Main Bot: Find a birthday gift under 50 dollars, book a table for two at 7 pm Saturday, and reply to my landlord. Don't decide anything for me yet
- Main Bot → Owner: Three product cards are up. Here's the restaurant I'll call, please choose Call. The reply to your landlord is drafted too
- Main Bot → Restaurant: Hi, I'm calling for a customer to book a table for two at 7 pm Saturday
- Restaurant → Main Bot: Sure. Any allergies?
- Main Bot → Owner: The restaurant is asking about allergies. Please answer in the question card
- Owner → Main Bot: No allergies. I'll take the second gift, I checked and paid the order card. Change the date in the email to next Monday, then I'll send it
:::

::: prompt
I have a few errands for you today. Handle them one by one:
1. [Errand one]
2. [Errand two]
3. [Errand three]
For each, stop at the step that needs a payment, a call, or a send, and give me the card or draft. At the end, send me a list of where each one is waiting.
:::

## Community picks: letting your Bot run errands {#community}

Here are a few ways to hand off errands. The first two come from the official docs' advice; the last three build on the official docs and are offered for reference.

### Lead with the result, then the limits {#c1}

The official advice: lead with the result you need, not a detailed imitation of every click. Link the source of truth, say what the Bot may change and what needs approval, and ask for evidence such as links, screenshots, or a short action log.

### Start a thread when the job changes {#c2}

The official advice: when a conversation has turned into a different long-lived job, start a thread or a new Bot instead of letting one chat grow forever. Errands can go to a Bot of their own.

### Write the script before the call {#c3}

Put the task, what you'll accept, your limits, and what not to share into one message, have the Bot repeat it back, then choose Call.

### Leave price watching to a routine {#c4}

Besides Notify me, you can ask a Bot to set up a routine that checks a few candidates' prices and tells you when one drops to your target, without ordering anything.

### Check names and times after a call {#c5}

After a booking or a change by phone, check that the Bot's summary includes the confirmed name, date, and time. If it doesn't, open the call card and read the transcript, and confirm again if needed.

## Exercise for this issue {#exercise}

Spend 15 minutes handing your Bot a small errand.

::: steps
- Pick something | Use the prompt from Tip 1 to have the Bot pick something you've been meaning to buy. Look at the product cards only; don't order.
- Set the rules | Use the prompt from "When to stop and let you decide" to give your Bot errand rules.
- Draft one email | Use the prompt from Tip 5 to get an email draft, and look at the buttons on the draft card.
- Practice redirecting | While it's working, send a new message that changes the request, and see how it adjusts.
:::
